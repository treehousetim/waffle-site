If you have been to the studio's home page, you will have seen the circle that breathes. It breathes in for four seconds and out for six. That rhythm is not decoration: it is one of the most useful things we teach, and you can take it anywhere.

## Why the out-breath matters

Your breath and your nervous system talk to each other in both directions. When you are anxious, your breathing speeds up and moves into your upper chest. But it works the other way, too: when you slow your breathing, and especially when you lengthen the exhale, you send your body a signal that it is safe to calm down.

You do not need to believe anything particular for this to work. Try it now, before you read any further: breathe in through your nose for a slow count of four, then out, gently, for a count of six. Do it three times.

Most people notice something. The shoulders drop a fraction. The jaw unclenches. The next thought arrives a little more slowly.

## The practice

1. **Sit or lie comfortably.** A chair is fine. Let your hands rest wherever they land.
2. **Breathe in through your nose for four.** Not a huge breath; an easy one. Let the belly soften as you breathe in.
3. **Breathe out for six.** Through the nose, or through softly pursed lips, as if you were cooling a spoonful of soup.
4. **Repeat for two or three minutes.** If you lose count, start again. Losing count is part of it.

If four and six feel long, try three and five, or even two and four. What matters is that the out-breath is a little longer than the in-breath, not the numbers themselves.

## When to use it

- before a difficult conversation or a phone call you have been putting off
- lying awake at three in the morning
- in traffic, in a waiting room, in the queue at the post office
- at the start of a yoga practice at home, to arrive

It is especially good before sleep. Ten rounds lying on your back, then roll onto your side and let the counting go.

## When to go gently

Breathing practices are generally very safe, but a few cautions are worth knowing. If counting makes you more anxious, not less, drop the numbers and simply let each exhale be slow and soft. If you feel light-headed, you are probably breathing in too deeply; make the in-breath smaller. And if you have a respiratory or heart condition, or are pregnant, keep everything easy and never hold the breath.
