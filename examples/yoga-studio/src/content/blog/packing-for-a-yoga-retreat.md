Every retreat I have led has had at least one person arrive with a suitcase large enough for a month, and at least one with a carrier bag and the wrong shoes. Here is the list I send everyone who comes away with us, whether for a weekend on the Alder or five days in the hills.

## For practice

- **Two or three sets of comfortable clothes.** You will practise twice a day, and things take a while to dry in a lodge.
- **Warm layers.** Morning practice on a deck is beautiful, and chilly. A fleece, a long-sleeved top, warm socks.
- **Your own mat, if you love it.** We bring mats, blocks, straps, bolsters and blankets for everyone, so it is entirely optional.
- **A shawl or light blanket.** For meditation, for the end of yin, for wrapping yourself in on the deck afterwards.

## For everything else

- **Walking shoes that are already broken in.** The paths upriver are lovely and occasionally muddy.
- **Something for the water.** Swimwear in summer, and an old pair of shoes for paddling. The lodge has kayaks.
- **A waterproof.** It is Oregon.
- **A book you have been meaning to read.** There is a great deal of unscheduled time on our retreats, by design.
- **A notebook.** People are often surprised by how much they want to write things down.
- **Any medication you need,** and a note of any allergies for the kitchen. Tell us about dietary needs when you book; the cooks are very good.

## What to leave at home

**Your laptop.** You know this already. If you genuinely cannot be out of contact, bring your phone and agree with yourself in advance when you will check it. Once a day, in your room, is plenty.

**Your expectations of yourself.** You do not need to come to every session. You do not need to be good at meditation. You do not need to come home transformed. Many people spend the first day of a retreat simply catching up on sleep, and that is entirely the point.

**Anything fancy.** Nobody dresses for dinner. The long table on the porch is the same in jeans as in anything else.

## What a day looks like

Our retreats keep a gentle shape. A slow vinyasa practice before breakfast, then the day is yours: walk, paddle, read, nap, sit by the water. In the late afternoon there is yin or restorative, then supper together, then quiet. On the five-day retreats there is usually one longer walk with a picnic, for anyone who wants it.

That rhythm, two practices a day with a great deal of space between them, is what makes a retreat different from a week of classes. The practice gets a chance to sink in.

## A last suggestion

Plan a quiet day for when you get home. The return from a retreat can be abrupt: emails, washing, the noise of the world. If you can, give yourself a gentle morning before going back to work, and maybe come to a class that week to carry a little of the stillness with you.
