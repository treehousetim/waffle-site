There is a look people give a yoga block the first time I hand them one. It is the look of someone being offered stabilisers on a bicycle. Surely, it says, proper yoga does not need a foam brick.

Here is what I tell them: props are not for people who cannot do the pose. Props are for doing the pose well.

## A block brings the floor closer

Take a standing forward fold. If your hamstrings are tight (and after a day at a desk, whose are not?) reaching for the floor pulls your spine into a hunch, your knees lock, and the stretch goes to the one place in your lower back that least needs it.

Put a block under each hand and something remarkable happens. Your spine can lengthen. Your knees can soften. The stretch arrives in the backs of the legs, where it was meant to be. The pose has not got easier. It has got *correct*.

## A strap makes your arms longer

In a seated forward fold, a strap around the feet lets you keep your chest open and your back long instead of grabbing at your toes. In shoulder stretches, it lets you work with the range you have instead of the range you wish you had. Nobody has ever been made more flexible by straining.

## A bolster holds you so you can let go

In yin and restorative, bolsters and blankets are what make the practice possible at all. A muscle that is working to hold you up cannot release. Prop the body properly and the nervous system gets the message that it is safe to stop guarding. That is where the real change happens.

## Who uses props here

Everyone. Maya, who has been teaching for seventeen years, uses two blocks in half of her standing poses. Iris builds restorative shapes with four bolsters and a pile of blankets. In my own practice I use a block under my hips in pigeon pose every single time, because my left hip has opinions about cycling.

If you look around one of our classes and see someone without props, they are probably new.

## How to get used to them

A few tips for your next class:

- **Take two blocks, a strap and a blanket before class starts,** even if you are not sure you will need them. Getting up mid-class to fetch them is the only awkward part.
- **Start with more height, not less.** It is easier to lower a block than to admit you needed one.
- **Try a pose both ways.** Once with the prop, once without. Notice where you feel it each time. You will usually find the propped version is the one doing what the teacher described.
- **Ask.** "How would you prop this for me?" is a question teachers love.

## The point of it all

Yoga is not a test you pass by touching your toes. It is a way of paying attention to your own body, as it is today. Props are one of the kindest tools we have for that. Use them generously, and without the slightest embarrassment.

They are free, by the way. Always will be.
