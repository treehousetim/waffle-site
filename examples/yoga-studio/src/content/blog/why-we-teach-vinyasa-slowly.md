Vinyasa has a reputation. Fast music, faster sequences, a room full of people who seem to have been born in a handstand. If that is what you expect from the word, our vinyasa classes may surprise you.

We teach flow slowly. Not gently, always (some of our evening classes are properly strong), but slowly. Here is why.

## One breath, one movement, really

Vinyasa means linking movement to breath. You reach up as you breathe in, fold as you breathe out. The breath sets the pace, and for most adults, at rest, a full breath takes four to six seconds.

In a lot of fast classes the movement sets the pace instead, and the breath scrambles to keep up. You can feel it: shallow, upper-chest breathing, a slight sense of panic. That is exercise, and there is nothing wrong with exercise, but it is not quite yoga.

In our classes, the breath leads. If the room's breathing starts to get ragged, the teacher slows down.

## Repetition is the point

We repeat sequences. A typical class builds one standing sequence over three or four rounds, adding a little each time. The first round is learning. The second is remembering. By the third, you can stop watching the teacher and start feeling your own body in the shapes.

That third round is where the practice lives. Without it, a class is a series of instructions to follow. With it, you have time to notice how your weight sits in your feet, what your jaw is doing, where the breath goes when you twist.

## A breath longer

We hold most poses for a breath or two longer than you might expect. Long enough to arrive, settle and adjust. Long enough to find the stability that makes a pose safe. And long enough, sometimes, to get a little uncomfortable, and to practise staying calm about it. That turns out to be useful well beyond the mat.

## Always ending on the floor

Every vinyasa class here spends its last fifteen minutes on the floor: hip openers, twists, a long final rest. Nobody leaves straight from a standing sequence with their heart still racing. The warmth you built has somewhere to go, and you walk out into Ferry Lane steadier than you walked in.

## Does slow mean easy?

No. Moving slowly is often harder than moving fast. Momentum hides a lot: weak spots, imbalances, a tendency to collapse into a joint. Slow removes the hiding places. Lowering from plank to the floor over a full exhale is a very different thing from dropping there.

You will get stronger. You will probably sweat. You will also, we hope, learn to stay with your breath while you work, which is perhaps the most valuable thing vinyasa can teach.

## Which vinyasa class to start with

If you are new to flow, try the Monday or Wednesday lunchtime class. It is forty-five minutes, every pose is named, and it is designed for people who want to move without being overwhelmed. The six forty-five classes are brisker, to wake you up. Wednesday evening, marked "some experience" on the schedule, includes arm balances and a longer flow.

Whichever you choose, take your time. The river is not going anywhere, and neither are we.
