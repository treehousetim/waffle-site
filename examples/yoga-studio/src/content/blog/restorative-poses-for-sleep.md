Most of the people who come to my restorative classes tell me, sooner or later, that they are not sleeping well. Restorative yoga is not a cure for insomnia, and if you have been struggling for a long time it is worth talking to your doctor. But it can be a lovely bridge between a busy day and a quiet night.

Here are five poses you can do at home, on the floor or on your bed, using cushions, pillows and blankets. Stay in each for five to ten minutes. Keep the lights low and your phone in another room.

## 1. Supported child's pose

Kneel with your knees wide and your big toes touching. Lay two pillows lengthways between your knees, and fold forward over them, turning your head to one side. Let your arms rest wherever they are comfortable. Halfway through, turn your head the other way.

This pose has a way of switching off the part of the brain that is still answering emails. Let your breath move into the back of your body.

## 2. Legs up the wall

Sit sideways next to a wall, then swing your legs up it as you lie back. Shuffle your hips close to the wall, or a little away from it if the backs of your legs complain. A folded blanket under the hips is nice; a pillow under the head is nicer.

If your feet tingle, bend the knees and let the soles of the feet rest on the wall. This is the pose I recommend most for tired legs after a day on your feet.

## 3. Supported reclined butterfly

Lie back over a pillow placed lengthways under your spine, with another under your head. Bring the soles of your feet together and let the knees fall open, and put a cushion under each knee so that nothing is straining. Rest your hands on your belly.

You should feel no stretch at all. If you do, add more support under the knees. This pose opens the front of the body gently, which can feel tender at first. Breathe slowly.

## 4. Side-lying rest

Lie on your left side with a pillow under your head, another between your knees, and if you like, one to hug. Let everything get heavy. This is the shape many people sleep in anyway, and that is the point: you are practising arriving into rest.

## 5. Supported savasana

Lie on your back with a pillow under your knees and a folded blanket under your head. Cover yourself with a blanket, because the body cools as it slows. Let the arms rest a little away from the sides, palms up.

Now breathe in for four and out for six, ten times. Then let the counting go, and just notice the breath arriving and leaving on its own.

## A few gentle rules

- **Comfort is everything.** If any part of you is holding on, add support until it is not.
- **Warmth matters.** Socks and a blanket, always.
- **Go slowly between poses.** Roll to your side and pause before you sit up.
- **Do less, not more.** Two poses for ten minutes each will do more than five rushed ones.
