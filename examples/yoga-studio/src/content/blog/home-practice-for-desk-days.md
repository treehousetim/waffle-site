I teach the lunchtime 45s for people who have exactly one hour away from their desk. Most of them arrive with the same body: hips folded, shoulders up, head poking forward towards a screen that is no longer there. If that sounds like you, here are ten minutes you can do at home, on a rug, at the end of a long day of sitting. No mat required.

Move slowly. Breathe through your nose. If anything pinches, come out a little.

## 1. Lying down, knees bent (one minute)

Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart. Rest your hands on your belly. Breathe in for four, out for six. Let the floor take the weight of your head and shoulders. This is where your nervous system gets the memo that the working day is over.

## 2. Knees side to side (one minute)

Still on your back, walk your feet a little wider than your hips. Let both knees drop slowly to the right, then back through the middle and to the left, like windscreen wipers. Let your head turn the other way if that feels good. Slow, easy, no forcing.

## 3. Cat and cow (two minutes)

Come onto your hands and knees. As you breathe in, let your belly drop and your chest lift, looking forward. As you breathe out, round your back towards the ceiling and let your head hang. Keep it smooth, and let the breath set the pace. Sitting all day keeps the spine in one shape; this reminds it there are others.

## 4. Low lunge (one minute each side)

From all fours, step your right foot forward between your hands and lower your left knee to the floor, with something soft under it. Bring your hands to your right thigh and let your hips sink gently forward until you feel the front of the left hip open. This is the stretch every desk worker needs most, because sitting shortens exactly these muscles. Switch sides.

## 5. Thread the needle (one minute each side)

Back on all fours, slide your right arm under your left, palm up, and lower your right shoulder and the side of your head to the floor. Your left hand can stay where it is or reach forward. Breathe into the back of your right shoulder. Switch sides. Your neck will thank you.

## 6. Supported forward fold (one minute)

Sit with your legs out in front of you, knees as bent as you need them, with a cushion or rolled blanket under your knees. Fold forward over your legs and rest your forehead on a pillow or stack of books. This is a yin shape: no straining, just staying. Let the back of the body lengthen on each exhale.

## Finish

Lie down again for as long as you have. Even thirty seconds is worth it.

## Make it a habit

The trick with home practice is not discipline, it is ease. Leave a cushion and a blanket somewhere visible. Practise at the same time each day: straight after work, before dinner, before bed. Ten minutes most days will do more for a desk-bound body than one heroic class a fortnight.

And when you want company and a teacher, the lunchtime vinyasa on Monday and Wednesday is forty-five minutes, every pose is named, and you can be back at your desk in time to look as if you never left.
