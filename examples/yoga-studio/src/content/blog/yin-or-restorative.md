People ask me this every week, usually while looking at the schedule with a slightly worried expression. Both classes are slow. Both happen mostly on the floor. Both involve a lot of bolsters. So what is the difference, and which one should you come to?

## The short answer

In **yin**, there is a gentle stretch, and you stay with it. In **restorative**, there is no stretch at all: the props hold you so completely that you can let go of everything.

Yin is a practice of patience. Restorative is a practice of rest. They overlap, but they are not the same.

## What happens in a yin class

A yin class has perhaps eight poses in it, each held for three to five minutes. The aim is a mild, spreading sensation in the deeper tissues around the joints: hips, spine, the backs of the legs. You are asked to find your edge, then back off a little, then stay.

Staying is the hard part. Around the second minute your mind will usually offer you something more interesting to think about, or suggest that you have been here long enough. Noticing that, and staying anyway, is most of what yin teaches.

Yin suits you if:

- your hips, lower back or hamstrings are stiff
- you do a lot of running, cycling, paddling or sitting
- you want to practise being still, and you are willing to find it uncomfortable at first

## What happens in a restorative class

A restorative class has five or six poses in an hour and a quarter. Each one is built for you, carefully, out of bolsters, blankets and blocks, so that every part of you is supported and nothing needs holding. Then you stay for eight, ten, twelve minutes.

There is no edge to find. If you notice any stretch at all, we add another blanket. The aim is for your nervous system to get the message that it can stand down. Breathing slows. Shoulders drop. Quite often, someone snores.

Restorative suits you if:

- you are tired, stressed, or not sleeping well
- you are recovering from illness, injury or a hard season of life
- you have forgotten what rest feels like

## How to choose on the day

Ask yourself one question before you look at the schedule: *do I want to be stretched, or held?*

If the answer is stretched, come to yin. If it is held, come to restorative. If the honest answer is "I do not know, I am just exhausted", come to restorative. Exhaustion is not a good state to find an edge from.

## Can I do both?

Yes, and many of our regulars do. A common rhythm is yin once a week to keep the body open, restorative once a week (often our candlelit Friday class) to keep the mind sane, and something more active in between.

## A note on discomfort

In yin, a dull, diffuse sensation is fine. Sharp, hot, pinching or electric sensations are not, in any class. Come out a little, and let the teacher help you prop it differently. There is always another way into a pose.

In restorative, any discomfort at all means the props need changing. Please tell us. I would rather spend five minutes rebuilding your shape than have you spend fifteen gritting your teeth in it.

Whichever you choose, bring socks and a warm layer. You will cool down while you are still, and nobody rests well when they are cold.
