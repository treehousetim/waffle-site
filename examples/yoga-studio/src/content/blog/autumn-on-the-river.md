Every year there is a morning in early October when I unlock the boathouse for the six forty-five and the river has disappeared. Mist sits on the water so thickly that the far bank is gone, and the only sound is the heron, somewhere out there, complaining about it.

That is when I know autumn has properly arrived, and that our classes will start to change.

## What changes at the studio

You might not notice it at first. The schedule stays the same. But over the next few weeks, the teachers will quietly begin to adjust what happens inside each class.

**Longer warm-ups.** Cold bodies need more time. Early classes will spend a few extra minutes on gentle spinal movement before anything asks much of the hamstrings.

**More grounding.** Standing poses that root down through the legs, like warrior two and wide-legged folds, come forward. Lots of balancing on one leg, less leaping about.

**More time on the floor.** Evening classes will lean further towards long holds, twists and hip openers. Yin classes fill up. The candlelit Friday restorative gets its waitlist back.

**The heating goes on.** Not hot, ever, but warm enough that nobody needs to keep their jumper on through sun salutations.

## Why practise with the seasons

You do not have to believe anything in particular about energy or elements for this to make sense. Autumn days are shorter and darker, sleep patterns shift, and many people feel a pull towards slowing down. Practice that works with that pull, rather than against it, simply feels better.

There is also something steadying about noticing the year turn. Coming to the same room, at the same time, while the view through the windows changes from green to gold to bare branches, is a small way of paying attention to time passing. We could all use more of that.

## Autumn at home

If you practise at home, a few suggestions for the season:

- **Practise in the morning light if you can.** Even ten minutes by a window helps with the darkening days.
- **Make it warm.** Socks for the slow parts, a blanket for the end.
- **Choose slower sequences.** A few sun salutations to warm up, then mostly long, grounded holds.
- **Lengthen your rest.** Five minutes of savasana becomes ten.
- **Breathe out for longer.** In for four, out for six, as often as you remember.

## Coming up this autumn

A few dates for the diary. The Slow Saturday afternoon of yin and sound with Iris is in early November, and the three-week breath course with Nell starts the week after. On the longest night of the year, the twenty-first of December, there is a candlelit restorative in the studio: two hours of lying very still while the river goes by in the dark.

And if you have been meaning to start, there is no better time than now. The mornings may be darker, but the boathouse is warm, the kettle is on, and the heron, eventually, always comes back.
