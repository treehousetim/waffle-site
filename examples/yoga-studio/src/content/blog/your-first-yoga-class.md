Almost everyone who walks into the boathouse for the first time is a little nervous. They worry they will be the stiffest person in the room, or that they will not know what to do, or that everyone will be watching. So here is what really happens, from the moment you open the door.

## Before you arrive

Wear something you can move in that will not need adjusting every five minutes: leggings or loose trousers, a T-shirt, a layer for the end. You practise barefoot, so socks are only for afterwards. Eat lightly an hour or two before if you can; a full stomach and a forward fold do not get on.

You do not need to bring anything. Mats, blocks, straps, bolsters and blankets are all here, and they are free. Bring a water bottle if you like one.

## At the door

Doors open twenty minutes before class. Shoes come off in the porch, phones go in the cubbies, and you tell whoever is at the desk that it is your first time. That is genuinely all you need to say. They will introduce you to the teacher, who will ask whether there is anything they should know: an injury, a pregnancy, a bad night's sleep.

Then they will help you set up. Somewhere near the back is a fine place to start, because you can see other people, but it is not a rule. Nobody has a regular spot they will glare at you for taking. Well, almost nobody.

## The class itself

Every class here begins slowly, usually lying down or sitting, with a few minutes to notice your breath. It sounds like nothing. It is the most important part.

Then the teacher will lead you through a sequence. In a beginners class every pose is named, shown and broken down, with options for different bodies. In other classes there is less explanation, but there are always options, and you can always do less.

A few things that help:

- **Rest whenever you want.** Child's pose, kneeling with your forehead down, is always allowed, in any class, for any reason.
- **Watch, then try.** It is fine to spend a pose looking at what other people are doing before having a go yourself.
- **Ask.** "Which leg?" is the most common question in any yoga class, and teachers like being asked.
- **Ignore the person next to you.** They are not watching you. They are wondering whether their own left hip is supposed to feel like that.

## The end

Every class ends with a long rest on your back (or your side, if you are pregnant or prefer it), with a blanket if you want one. Many people say this is the best part. You are allowed to fall asleep. The teacher will bring you back.

Afterwards there is tea. Stay a few minutes or slip away quietly; both are completely normal.

## The next day

You may feel muscles you had forgotten about. That is ordinary, and it fades within a day or two. Sharp pain is not ordinary, and is worth mentioning to the teacher next time.

The hardest class is the second one, because you have to decide to come back. If you can, book it before you leave the first. The intro offer makes it easy: three weeks of every class for $40, so you can try beginners, restorative and anything else that catches your eye without counting the cost.

> You do not have to be good at this. You only have to be here.

I say it at the start of almost every beginners class, and I mean it every time. See you on the mat.
