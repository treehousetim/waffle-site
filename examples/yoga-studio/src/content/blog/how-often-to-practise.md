It is one of the first questions people ask after the intro offer, usually while looking hopefully at the monthly membership and doubtfully at their diary. How often do I really need to come?

The honest answer is: more often than you think you have time for, and less often than you think you should.

## Once a week

Once a week is enough to feel the difference. The hour itself does you good, every time: you leave calmer, looser, a little taller. Over months, a weekly class keeps the body moving in directions daily life does not ask it to, and gives you a regular hour of not being in a hurry.

What once a week will not usually do is change things quickly. Flexibility and strength return slowly between classes, so each week begins a little behind where the last one finished. That is fine. Plenty of our students have come once a week for years and would not be without it.

## Two or three times a week

This is where most people notice real change. With two or three classes a week, the body starts to remember from one class to the next. Hamstrings stop resetting. Poses that were puzzling become familiar. Sleep often improves.

A good rhythm is one more active class and one slower one: vinyasa on Monday, yin on Thursday, say. They balance each other, and they teach different things.

## Every day

A daily practice is a wonderful thing, but it does not have to mean a daily class. Many of us practise for ten or fifteen minutes at home most mornings, and come to the studio two or three times a week. The home practice keeps the thread; the classes keep it honest.

Ten minutes is plenty for a home practice. A few rounds of cat and cow, a forward fold, a lunge on each side, and a few minutes lying down with your knees bent and your breath slow. If you only have three minutes, lie down and breathe. It counts.

## Consistency, not intensity

What matters most is not how hard you practise but how regularly. A gentle class every week for a year will do more for you than a month of punishing daily sessions followed by six months of guilt.

So choose a rhythm you can actually keep. Look at your real week, not your ideal one. If Tuesday evenings and Saturday mornings are the only times that are reliably yours, those are your yoga days. Book them before anything else gets them.

## Listening to the body

Some weeks you will want more. Some weeks you will want nothing but restorative. Both are information. One of the quieter skills yoga teaches is telling the difference between "I do not feel like it" (often worth overriding) and "I really need rest" (always worth listening to).

If you are not sure which it is, come anyway and take the gentlest options. You can always lie down at the back. Nobody will mind, and you will probably leave glad you came.

## A suggestion

During your intro weeks, try coming three times a week, to three different classes. At the end of the three weeks you will know which styles you love, which times work, and how your body responds to regular practice. Then choose the rhythm that fits, and the pass or membership that matches it. The desk will happily help you do the sums.
