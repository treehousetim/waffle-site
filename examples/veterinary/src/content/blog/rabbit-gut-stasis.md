If you take one thing away from this post, let it be this: **a rabbit who stops eating is an emergency.** Not tomorrow. Today.

Rabbits' guts are designed to keep moving all the time. When they slow down or stop, a condition we call gut stasis, gas and fluid build up, bacteria get out of balance, the rabbit feels painful and stops eating, and the gut slows further. It can become life-threatening within a day.

## The early signs

Rabbits are prey animals and hide pain very well, so the signs are subtle at first:

- eating less, or ignoring favourite foods and treats,
- fewer droppings, smaller droppings, or none at all,
- sitting hunched in a corner, reluctant to move,
- teeth grinding (a loud grinding is a sign of pain, unlike the soft purr-like chatter of a happy rabbit),
- a bloated or tight-feeling belly,
- not coming to greet you.

**If your rabbit hasn't eaten or passed droppings for 12 hours, call us,** or the after-hours emergency hospital if we're closed.

## What causes it

Gut stasis is usually a sign that something else is wrong. The common triggers are:

- **Not enough hay.** Fibre keeps the gut moving. A diet heavy in pellets, muesli or treats is the biggest single risk.
- **Dental disease.** Rabbits' teeth grow all their lives. Overgrown or spiky back teeth make eating painful, so they eat less.
- **Pain or stress** from another illness, an injury, a new home, a predator nearby, or losing a companion.
- **Dehydration,** especially in hot weather.
- **Swallowed fur,** during a heavy moult, combined with too little fibre.

## What we do

When you call, we'll ask you to come straight in. We examine the belly, check the teeth, take the temperature (a low temperature is a worrying sign) and often take an X-ray to look for a blockage, which needs very different treatment.

Most rabbits with stasis need:

- **pain relief,** because pain alone can keep the gut stopped,
- **fluids,** by injection or a drip,
- **medicines** to get the gut moving, if there's no blockage,
- **syringe feeding** with a special recovery food,
- **warmth,** quiet, and treatment for whatever caused it in the first place.

Many rabbits go home the same day with medicines and syringe feeding to do at home. Some need to stay in our quiet exotics ward for a night or two. Bring their companion rabbit if they have one: bonded pairs recover better together.

## How to prevent it

**Hay, hay, and more hay.** About 85% of a rabbit's diet should be good-quality grass hay, available all the time, in a pile roughly the size of the rabbit every day. Add a handful of fresh greens, and only a small eggcup of pellets. No muesli.

Other things that help:

- fresh water in a bowl (rabbits drink more from bowls than bottles),
- lots of exercise and space to run,
- brushing during a moult,
- a dental check at every visit,
- a companion rabbit, for company and less stress.

## Learn your rabbit's normal

Get to know how much your rabbit usually eats and how many droppings appear each day. A quick look every morning and evening is all it takes, and it is how most of our owners catch stasis early enough to make treatment simple.
