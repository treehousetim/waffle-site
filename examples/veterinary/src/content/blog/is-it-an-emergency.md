When your pet is unwell, it can be hard to know whether you're overreacting or not reacting enough. Here's how we think about it on the phone, so you can too. And the most important line comes first.

**If you're worried, call.** You don't need to be sure. A nurse will listen, ask a few questions and tell you whether to come straight in, book today, or keep an eye on things.

## Go now: these can't wait

Call us as you set off (or the after-hours emergency hospital when we're closed) if your pet has:

- **trouble breathing:** noisy breathing, struggling, blue or grey gums, or a cat breathing with its mouth open,
- **collapsed,** or can't stand or walk,
- **a seizure** lasting more than three minutes, or more than one in a day,
- **a swollen, tight belly** with retching and nothing coming up, especially in a large, deep-chested dog,
- **a male cat straining** in the litter tray and producing little or nothing,
- **heavy bleeding** that doesn't slow with firm pressure,
- **been hit by a car** or had a bad fall or fight, even if they seem fine,
- **eaten something poisonous,** even if they seem well,
- **a rabbit** that hasn't eaten or passed droppings for 12 hours,
- **pale gums,** extreme weakness or a very cold body.

## Today: book a same-day visit

These usually aren't emergencies, but your pet should be seen within the day:

- vomiting more than two or three times, or with blood,
- diarrhoea that lasts more than a day, or with blood,
- not eating for a day (half a day for small pets),
- limping that doesn't improve after a few hours of rest,
- a swollen face, hives or lots of scratching after a sting or new food,
- a sore, red or cloudy eye, or a squinting eye,
- straining to pee, or peeing much more often,
- a wound, a bite, or a torn nail,
- being much quieter than normal.

We keep appointments free every day for exactly these. Call in the morning if you can.

## Soon: book a routine appointment

These are worth a check within a week or so:

- a new lump that isn't growing quickly,
- slowly drinking more, or gradually losing weight,
- itchy skin or ears,
- bad breath or a dirty-looking tooth,
- gradual stiffness, or slowing down on walks.

## What to do on the way

- **Call ahead,** so we can get ready and tell you what to do in the car.
- **Keep your pet still and warm.** Slide a towel or board under an injured dog to lift them. Put cats and small pets in a carrier.
- **Press firmly** on any bleeding with a clean cloth, and keep pressing.
- **Bring the evidence:** the packet, the plant, or a photo of what they ate.
- **Drive carefully.** Arriving safely matters more than arriving a minute sooner.

## A word on worry

We never mind a call that turns out to be nothing. Every vet and nurse here would far rather reassure you over the phone than see a pet a day later than they should have been. Trust your instinct: you know your pet better than anyone.

Save our number, and the after-hours number, in your phone now. They're both on our [emergency page](/emergency/), along with directions, so you won't have to search for them on a bad day.
