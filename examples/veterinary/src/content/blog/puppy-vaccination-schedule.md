Bringing a puppy home is a joy, and it comes with a lot of questions. The one we hear most at the front desk is some version of "when can we go for a walk?" The answer depends on the vaccine course, so here it is, week by week, with the reasons behind it.

## Why puppies need a course, not a single shot

A puppy is born with some protection from its mother, passed on in the first milk. Those maternal antibodies are wonderful for the first few weeks, but they fade at a different rate in every puppy, somewhere between about 6 and 16 weeks of age. While they are still around, they can block a vaccine from working.

We can't know exactly when your puppy's maternal protection runs out, so we vaccinate every three to four weeks until about 16 weeks old. Each dose is a chance to catch the moment the puppy's own immune system can respond. The final dose, at 16 weeks or a little later, is the one that matters most, which is why we never stop the course early.

## The schedule

| Age | What we give | What else happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 weeks | First core vaccine (distemper, parvovirus, adenovirus) | Full exam, worming, microchip |
| 11 to 12 weeks | Second core vaccine, plus leptospirosis if needed | Weight check, socialising tips |
| 15 to 16 weeks | Final core vaccine, rabies, second leptospirosis | Talk about spay or neuter |
| 1 year | First boosters | Adult wellness exam |

If your puppy came from a breeder or shelter with a first vaccine already given, bring the card and we will pick up the course where it left off.

## Core and lifestyle vaccines

**Core vaccines** are for every dog, everywhere: distemper, parvovirus and adenovirus (often given together in one injection), and rabies, which is also required by law.

**Lifestyle vaccines** depend on how your dog lives:

- **Leptospirosis** for dogs who drink from puddles, swim, hike or live near wildlife. Around Bramblewick, with the river and the wetlands, that is most dogs.
- **Kennel cough** (Bordetella) for dogs going to boarding, daycare, grooming or puppy classes.
- **Lyme disease** for dogs who spend a lot of time in long grass and woodland where ticks are common.

We will ask about your plans at the first visit and only recommend what fits.

## So, when can we walk?

About one week after the final puppy vaccine, so usually at around 17 weeks. That can feel like an age when you have a bouncy puppy at home, but parvovirus lives in soil and on pavements for months and is very hard on young dogs.

That does not mean keeping your puppy shut indoors. The weeks between 8 and 16 are the most important for socialising, and a puppy who misses them can grow into a fearful adult. In the meantime:

- **Carry your puppy** out to see the street, the shops, the traffic and the school run.
- **Invite healthy, vaccinated adult dogs** you know well to meet in your garden.
- **Have visitors over:** hats, beards, children, umbrellas, wheelchairs. Variety is the point.
- **Practise handling:** touch paws, ears and mouths gently, with a treat every time. Your vet will thank you.
- **Come to a happy visit:** pop in to step on the scales, have a fuss and get a treat.

## Side effects

Most puppies are a little sleepy for a day after a vaccine, and some have a small lump where the needle went in that fades over a week or two. Call us if your puppy has a swollen face, hives, vomiting, or is very flat. Those reactions are rare, and treatable, but we want to know.
