Cats are experts at looking fine. It is an old survival instinct: in the wild, a cat who looks unwell is a cat who gets eaten. So when an older cat finally looks ill, they have often been unwell for a while.

The trick is to notice the small changes. Here are the six we most want older cats' owners to watch for, from about eleven years of age onwards.

## 1. Drinking more

If you're filling the water bowl more often, finding your cat at the tap, or the litter is suddenly much wetter, tell us. More thirst in an older cat is one of the commonest early signs of kidney disease, an overactive thyroid or diabetes. All three are very treatable, and all three are easier to manage when caught early.

**Try this:** measure the water you put down in the morning, and what is left the next morning, for a few days.

## 2. Losing weight while eating well

A cat who is hungrier than ever and getting thinner very often has an overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism). It is common in older cats and usually treatable with tablets, a special diet or a one-off treatment. A cat who is eating less and losing weight needs a check too, often for teeth or kidneys.

**Try this:** run your hand along the spine and hips every week. If the bones feel sharper than they did, book a weigh-in. Nurse weigh-ins are free.

## 3. Changes in the litter tray

Peeing outside the tray, straining, going more often, or constipation are all worth a call. In older cats they are often caused by kidney disease, urinary infections, or arthritis that makes climbing into a high-sided tray painful.

**Try this:** offer a tray with one low side, and put one on every floor of the house.

## 4. A scruffy or matted coat

Older cats who stop grooming are usually telling us something hurts: stiff joints that make reaching their back difficult, or a sore mouth that makes licking unpleasant. A greasy, spiky or matted coat along the back and tail is a classic sign.

## 5. Not jumping up any more

Arthritis is extremely common in older cats, and very under-treated, because cats don't limp much. Instead, they stop jumping onto the bed, hesitate at the stairs, use the chair to get to the counter, or sleep in lower places. They may seem grumpy when picked up.

There is a lot we can do: pain relief made for cats, a newer monthly arthritis injection, weight loss if needed, and small changes at home like steps and ramps to favourite spots.

## 6. Yowling at night, or seeming lost

Some older cats start crying loudly at night, wander, or seem confused about where they are. This can be high blood pressure, an overactive thyroid, poor eyesight or hearing, or age-related changes in the brain. It is worth checking, because several of the causes are treatable, and there are ways to help with the rest.

## What a senior check involves

From around eleven, we suggest a check every six months. Six months in a cat's life is like two or three years in ours. A senior check adds:

- a careful look at teeth, joints, eyes and weight,
- a blood test for kidneys, thyroid and diabetes,
- a urine test,
- a blood pressure check, done calmly and with patience.
