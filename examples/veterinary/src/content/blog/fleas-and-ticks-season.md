Every spring the phone starts ringing with the same two questions: "Is this a tick?" and "Why is my cat scratching?" Here is our nurse's guide to keeping fleas and ticks off your pets, and what to do when they turn up anyway.

## When are they around?

Ticks love long grass, woodland edges and the riverside paths around Bramblewick, and they're most active from spring to autumn, though a mild winter keeps some going all year. Fleas never really go away: they love our centrally heated houses, so they breed all year round indoors.

That's why we recommend year-round protection for most dogs and cats, rather than just in summer.

## Fleas: the facts

Only about 5% of a flea problem lives on your pet. The other 95% is eggs, larvae and cocoons in carpets, sofas, pet beds and car seats. That's why one treatment rarely clears an infestation, and why it can take three months to be properly free of them.

**Signs of fleas:** scratching and nibbling, especially over the back and base of the tail; red, scabby skin; and "flea dirt", tiny black specks in the coat. To test a speck, put it on a damp white tissue: flea dirt turns reddish brown, because it's digested blood.

## Clearing a flea problem

1. **Treat every pet in the house,** cats, dogs and rabbits, with a product made for that species. A dog product can make a cat very ill, so never share.
2. **Treat the house.** Vacuum everywhere daily for two weeks, including under furniture and the car, and throw the vacuum contents away outside. A household flea spray kills eggs and larvae in carpets.
3. **Wash pet bedding** on a hot wash.
4. **Keep treating monthly.** New fleas will keep hatching from cocoons for weeks. That's normal and doesn't mean the treatment has failed.

## Ticks: the facts

Ticks climb onto pets brushing past grass, then bite and feed for several days, growing to the size of a coffee bean. They can carry infections, including Lyme disease, so it's worth removing them promptly and protecting pets who walk where ticks live.

**Check after every walk** in long grass or woodland: run your hands over your dog, especially the head, ears, armpits, between the toes and around the collar. A tick feels like a small, firm bump.

## How to remove a tick

- Use a **tick remover hook** (we have them at the front desk). Avoid squeezing with your fingers or tweezers, which can squash the tick.
- Slide the hook under the tick close to the skin, and **twist gently** several times until it comes away.
- **Don't** burn it, cover it in petroleum jelly or pull it straight off: those make it more likely to leave mouthparts behind or regurgitate into the bite.
- Clean the area, wash your hands, and keep an eye on the spot. A small red bump for a few days is normal.

If the bite gets very swollen, or your dog becomes lethargic, lame or off their food in the weeks after a tick bite, call us.

## Choosing protection

There are tablets, spot-ons and collars, and the right one depends on your pet's lifestyle, species, age and whether there are children or cats in the house. Some protect against fleas and ticks; some add worms. We'll help you choose at your pet's yearly exam, and dose it to their current weight.

Buy treatments from your vet or a reputable pharmacy. Cheap online imports are sometimes fake, and some contain doses that are dangerous for small pets.
