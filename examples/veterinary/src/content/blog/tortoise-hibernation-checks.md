Every autumn our exotics room fills up with tortoises being weighed, measured and checked before their long winter sleep. Hibernation is natural and healthy for many species, but only for a tortoise that is well, at a good weight, and kept at a safe, steady temperature.

## Is your tortoise a hibernating species?

Mediterranean tortoises, such as Hermann's, spur-thighed and marginated tortoises, and Horsfield's tortoises usually hibernate. Many tropical species, including red-footed and leopard tortoises, must not hibernate at all. If you are not sure what species you have, bring a photo or the tortoise itself and we will tell you.

## Book a check in late summer

We like to see hibernating tortoises in late August or September, at least six weeks before they wind down. We weigh and measure them, check eyes, nose, mouth and shell, listen to the lungs, and test a droppings sample for worms. A tortoise that is underweight, has a runny nose or is carrying worms should not hibernate this year.

## Winding down

For two to three weeks before hibernation, a tortoise needs to empty its gut, because undigested food can ferment during the winter.

- Stop feeding, but keep offering shallow baths of lukewarm water every other day.
- Gradually shorten the hours under the heat lamp and lower the temperature.
- Weigh your tortoise weekly, and write it down.

## A safe hibernation

The safest place is a fridge kept just for the tortoise, at a steady 5°C (41°F), checked daily with a thermometer. A box in a frost-free garage can work, but temperatures swing too much in many garages. Below 1°C a tortoise can freeze and go blind; above 10°C it burns through its reserves too fast.

Weigh your tortoise every week or two. A healthy tortoise loses about 1% of its body weight a month. If it loses much more, or passes urine, wake it up and call us.

## When to wake them early

Wake your tortoise, warm it gently and call us if it loses more than about 10% of its starting weight, passes urine, has a runny nose or swollen eyes, or if the temperature has dipped near freezing. Waking early is always safer than pressing on with a tortoise that is struggling.

## Waking up

Most tortoises hibernate for 8 to 12 weeks. When it is time, warm them gradually, offer a bath straight away, and expect them to eat within a week. A tortoise that has not eaten within a week of waking needs a check.
