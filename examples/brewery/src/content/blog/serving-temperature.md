Every summer someone asks why their pint is not "frosty". It is a reasonable question. Decades of advertising have taught us that the colder the beer, the better. For mass-market lager that may even be true, because cold hides flavour, and some beers are better with their flavour hidden.

Ours are not, so we pour them a little warmer than you might expect, and each style at its own temperature.

## What cold does

Cold numbs the tongue and keeps aroma locked in the glass. The compounds that make a beer smell of bread, citrus, pine, coffee or fruit are volatile: they need a little warmth to rise out of the beer and reach your nose, which is where most of what we call taste really happens.

Chill a beer to 34°F and it will be refreshing, carbonated and, mostly, cold. Let the same beer come up to 45°F and it starts to tell you what it is.

## Our serving temperatures

| Style | Serve at | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pilsner, light lagers | 40–44°F | Crisp and refreshing, with the bready malt still showing |
| Hazy pale ale, IPA | 44–46°F | Lets the hop aroma lift without the bitterness turning harsh |
| Saison | 45–48°F | The pepper and fruit from the yeast come forward |
| Amber, brown, rye | 48–52°F | Caramel and toast need a little warmth |
| Stout, on nitro | 50°F | The roast and chocolate open up, and the body softens |
| Cask ale | 52–55°F | Cellar temperature, the way it was always served |
| Imperial stout | 55°F and rising | Let it warm in your hand; it changes as you drink |

Our cold room runs at 36°F, and the lines and glasses do the rest. The stout and the cask lines have their own controls.

## At home

Most home fridges sit between 35°F and 38°F, which is a little cold for anything but a light lager. Some simple fixes:

- **Take it out early.** Fifteen minutes on the counter before you open a can of IPA, twenty for a stout.
- **Use the door.** It is usually the warmest part of the fridge, and a decent place for ales.
- **Do not chill the glass in the freezer.** A frosted glass knocks the beer colder still, and the ice crystals make it foam and go flat faster. A clean glass rinsed in cold water is perfect.
- **Hold the glass by the bowl** with darker, stronger beers. Your hand is a gentle heater.

## A test worth doing

Pour two small glasses of the same beer, one straight from the fridge and one that has sat out for twenty minutes. Smell both, then taste both. Most people are surprised at how much they have been missing.
