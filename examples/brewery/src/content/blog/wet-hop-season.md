Almost every hop that goes into beer has been dried. Freshly picked hop cones are about 80% water, and left wet they spoil in a day or two, so farms kiln them down to around 10% moisture, press them into bales or pellets, and ship them all year.

Once a year, for a few weeks at the end of summer, we get to skip that step. A wet hop beer, also called a fresh hop beer, is brewed with hops that were on the bine that morning. The flavour is different from anything dried hops can give: green, grassy, a little like fresh-cut herbs and citrus peel, softer and less resinous. It does not keep, and it cannot be repeated out of season. That is the point of it.

## The farm

Our hops come from Hollis Farm, about forty minutes up the valley, which grows a few acres of Cascade and Chinook on twenty-foot trellises. Their harvest window is set by the plants, not by us. When the cones are papery on the outside, springy when squeezed and smell sharply of citrus when crushed, they are ready, and they will not wait for a convenient Tuesday.

This year the call came on a Thursday evening: picking at six the next morning.

## The clock

The rule we work to is simple: hops in the kettle within six hours of being cut. Every hour after that, they lose aroma and start to smell like wet hay.

- **5:00am.** Ruth starts the mash at the brewery so the kettle will be ready by mid-morning.
- **6:00am.** Two of us are at the farm with clean tote bags and a van. The bines are cut and fed through the farm's picker, which strips the cones.
- **8:30am.** About 90 pounds of wet cones are in the van. For comparison, a batch with dried hops would use around 15 pounds. Wet hops are mostly water, so you need five to six times as much by weight.
- **9:45am.** Back on Foundry Row. The wort is already boiling.
- **10:15am.** The first hops go in. The building smells like a field.

## In the kettle

Because they are so bulky, wet hops are a nuisance to brew with. They soak up wort, they clog things, and they fill the whirlpool to the brim. We use most of them late: a large addition at the end of the boil and the rest in the whirlpool, where the aromatics are captured without boiling them off.

The base beer is deliberately simple, a pale ale with a touch of light crystal malt and enough body to carry the hops. Anything bolder would bury the fresh character we spent the morning chasing.

## In the glass

Wet Bine Harvest Ale ferments for about ten days and goes straight to keg. It is at its best in its first three weeks and noticeably faded by six, so we brew one batch, pour it until it is gone, and do not apologise for running out.

This year's is on tap now, with a final keg going on for last call on the 23rd. If you have never had a fresh hop beer, this is the time of year to try one, ours or anyone's. In a month you will have to wait another eleven.
