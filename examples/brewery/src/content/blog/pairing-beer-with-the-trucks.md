We do not have a kitchen. We have something better: a different food truck parked outside the loading dock five days a week, run by people who are much better cooks than we are. The question we get asked most at the bar, after "what is new?", is "what should I drink with this?".

A few principles first, then the specifics.

## Three rules of thumb

1. **Match strength with strength.** Delicate food wants a delicate beer; rich, smoky or spicy food can stand up to something bolder.
2. **Bitterness cuts fat; carbonation scrubs the palate.** That is why a crisp pilsner or a lively saison is such a good partner for anything fried.
3. **Hops amplify heat.** If the food is very spicy, a highly bitter IPA will make it feel hotter. Choose something maltier, or lean into it if you like the burn.

## Wednesday: Ferry Street Pierogi Wagon

Potato and cheese, sauerkraut and mushroom, all pan-fried in butter with onions.

**Drink:** Pour Line Pilsner. This is the pairing the style was made for: soft bready malt alongside the dough, a crisp dry finish and a bitter snap to cut through the butter. Ferryman's Rye is the alternative if you have gone for the kielbasa; the rye spice and caramel love smoked sausage.

## Thursday: La Plancha

Tacos al pastor, birria with consommé for dipping, grilled corn with chili and lime.

**Drink:** Tamsen Haze. Its tropical fruit plays with the pineapple in the al pastor, and its soft bitterness will not fan the chili. With the rich, beefy birria, try the cask brown if it is on: its nutty malt is like an extra layer of toasted tortilla.

## Friday: Smoke Signal BBQ

Brisket, pulled pork, smoked wings and a mac and cheese that should be illegal.

**Drink:** Cupola IPA, if you like your pairings bold. The resin and grapefruit stand up to the smoke and the bitterness slices through the fat. For something gentler, Ferryman's Rye: caramel against the bark, spice against the rub. And with the burnt ends, our stout, which tastes like it was invented for them.

## Saturday: Ember & Crust

Wood-fired pizza from a trailer oven, blistered crusts, simple toppings.

**Drink:** Bellows Saison. The pepper and lemon flatter tomato and basil, and the high carbonation lifts the cheese off your tongue between bites. With anything topped with spicy salami, the pilsner again.

## Sunday: the guest truck

We rotate a guest truck on Sundays; the events page says who. If in doubt, order a flight of four tasters and treat it as an experiment. It is the most fun you can have with a paper plate.

Whatever you choose, the bartenders will always let you taste first. And if you find a pairing that we have missed, tell us. We will probably steal it for next month's chalkboard.
