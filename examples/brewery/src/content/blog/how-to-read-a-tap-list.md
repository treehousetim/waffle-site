Our tap board carries a lot of information in a small space, and we know not everyone wants to ask what every abbreviation means while a queue forms behind them. Here is how to read it.

## The number on the left

That is the tap number. Taps 1 to 6 are usually our core range, in roughly the same order every week, lightest to darkest. Taps 7 to 12 are where the specials, one-offs and guest pours live. If you liked something last time, the tap number is the quickest way to ask for it again.

## The name and the style

The style tells you more than the name does. A few we pour often:

- **Pilsner** and other lagers: crisp, clean, bready and dry. Cold-fermented and long-conditioned.
- **IPA**: hop-forward. A *West Coast* IPA is clear, dry and bitter; a *hazy* or *New England* IPA is cloudy, soft and juicy.
- **Saison**: a dry, peppery, lively farmhouse ale from Belgium.
- **Stout**: dark, roasty and often smooth. An *oatmeal* stout has extra body; an *imperial* stout is much stronger.
- **Sour**: tart, often fruited. Brewed with bacteria as well as yeast.

## ABV

Alcohol by volume, as a percentage. Most of our beers sit between 4.5% and 7%. Anything over 8% is poured in a smaller glass, and we mean it when we say to take it slowly: our barrel-aged imperial stout is over 11%, which makes an 8 oz snifter roughly the same as two pints of pilsner.

## IBU

International bitterness units, a measure of how much hop bitterness is in the beer. It is a rough guide, not a rule. Below 20 is barely bitter, 30 to 40 is a firm, balanced bitterness, and 60 or more is assertive. Malt sweetness changes how bitterness tastes, so a 40 IBU stout can taste less bitter than a 35 IBU pilsner. A dash means we did not measure it, usually for sours and ciders, where it means little.

## The flags

- **New**: tapped in the last week.
- **Limited**: one batch or one keg. When it is gone, it is gone.
- **Nitro**: poured with a mix of nitrogen and carbon dioxide instead of carbon dioxide alone. The bubbles are much smaller, so the beer feels creamy and pours with a cascading head. Give it a minute to settle.
- **Cask**: naturally carbonated in the keg, unfiltered, pulled by hand from a beer engine and served a little warmer, around 54°F. Softer, rounder and less fizzy than you may expect, in the best way.

## The pours

Every beer comes as a **taster** (5 oz), and most as a **half** (10 oz) and a **pint** (16 oz). Stronger beers come in a tulip or snifter instead. Stouts and casks come in a 20 oz imperial pint, because that is how they were meant to be drunk.

A **flight** is any four tasters on a paddle. It is the best way to find your beer on a first visit.

## And if in doubt

Ask for a taste. Before you buy, any beer on the board, any time. The bartenders would far rather pour you a splash of three beers than watch you struggle through a pint you do not like.
