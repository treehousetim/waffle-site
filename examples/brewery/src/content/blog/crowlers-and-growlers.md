Beer is at its best at the bar, poured from a keg that has been cold since the day it was filled. But not everyone can drink every pint on the premises, and we would rather you took some home than missed a limited release. Here is how to get the most out of each way we sell beer to go.

## The enemies of fresh beer

Three things make beer go stale:

1. **Oxygen.** It slowly turns hop aroma papery and dull and malt flavour cardboard-like. Hoppy beers suffer first.
2. **Warmth.** Every chemical change in beer speeds up when it is warm. A week at room temperature can age a beer like a month in the fridge.
3. **Light.** Ultraviolet light reacts with hop compounds to make the "skunky" smell you may know from green bottles. Cans and dark glass protect against it.

Everything below is about keeping those three out.

## Cans

Our 16 oz cans are filled on the canning line in the brewery, purged with carbon dioxide before filling and sealed immediately. They block light completely and let in almost no oxygen.

- **Keep them cold.** Straight into the fridge when you get home.
- **Hoppy beers within a month or two.** Cupola IPA and Tamsen Haze are best within six weeks of the date on the bottom.
- **Lagers and stouts keep longer**, three to four months, but are still best fresh.

## Crowlers

A crowler is a 32 oz can filled from the tap at the bar and sealed with a lid on a small seaming machine while you watch. We fill from the bottom and cap on foam to push out as much air as we can.

- **Treat it like a can.** Cold, upright, out of the sun.
- **Drink it within two weeks.** A crowler is filled more slowly than a canning line fills a can, so a little more oxygen gets in. It will be fine for a fortnight, and best in the first few days.
- **Once open, finish it.** 32 oz is two good pints. Share it.

## Growlers

A growler is a 64 oz glass or steel jug, and the oldest way of taking beer home from a brewery. We will fill any clean growler with a screw cap.

- **Bring it clean.** Rinse it with hot water as soon as you have finished it and let it dry upside down. We cannot fill a growler that smells of the last beer, or of anything else.
- **Drink it within a couple of days.** A growler is filled from a tap and capped by hand, with more air in it than a can or crowler. Unopened, it keeps for two or three days; once opened, it goes flat within a day.
- **Keep it out of the light**, especially if it is clear glass.

## Which to choose

For tonight, a growler is fine. For the weekend, get a crowler. For next month, buy cans. And for the barrel-aged imperial stout, which we bottle in 750 ml bottles for the very reason that it ages well, buy two: one for now and one to forget about in a cool cupboard until next winter.

Everything we sell to go is for over-21s only, with ID, and Ohio law says it must stay sealed until you are home.
