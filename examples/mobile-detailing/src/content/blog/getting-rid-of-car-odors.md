A car is a small, sealed box that bakes in the sun and carries food, wet coats, sports kit, dogs and children. It is no surprise that it starts to smell. What surprises people is that air fresheners make it worse: they add a second smell on top of the first, and the first one keeps growing underneath.

To get rid of an odor, you have to find what is making it and remove that. Here is where to look.

## Where smells live

**Carpets and mats.** Spilled milk, juice, coffee and melted snow soak through the carpet into the padding underneath, where they sour slowly. A wet carpet that never dried properly is also where mildew starts.

**Cloth seats.** Fabric and the foam beneath it hold sweat, food and pet smells. You cannot vacuum them out: they are in the fibers, not on them.

**Vents and the cabin filter.** The air conditioning evaporator is cold and damp, and in summer it can grow a film of mold that blows a musty smell into the cabin every time the fan comes on. An old cabin air filter makes it worse.

**The headliner.** Smoke, especially, rises and settles into the soft fabric on the roof.

**Under the seats.** Every family car we clean has something under a seat. Usually it is a french fry. Sometimes it is worse.

## How we deal with each

Our [Interior Deep Clean](/packages/interior-deep-clean/) is built around removing sources, in order.

1. **Everything out.** Mats out, compressed air through every seam, rail and vent to blow out what a vacuum cannot reach.
2. **Hot-water extraction.** For carpets, mats and cloth seats, we spray hot water and a gentle shampoo into the fabric and immediately pull it back out with a powerful vacuum. It flushes the fibers instead of just cleaning the surface, and removes most of the water so it dries in hours.
3. **Steam.** Dry steam sanitizes hard surfaces, cupholders, consoles and door pockets without soaking electronics.
4. **Enzyme treatment.** For organic smells (food, sweat, pets, the occasional car-sick child), we apply an enzyme cleaner that breaks down the proteins causing the smell rather than masking them.
5. **Dry, properly.** Doors open, fans on, and a final check that nothing under the carpet is still damp.

We will also tell you if your cabin air filter needs changing. It is a cheap part and often the cause of a musty vent smell.

## What you can do between visits

- **Deal with spills the same day.** Blot, do not rub. A damp microfiber and a little upholstery cleaner while it is fresh saves a lot of work later.
- **Use rubber mats in winter**, so snow and slush melt into a tray you can empty, not into the carpet.
- **Run the fan without the air conditioning** for the last couple of minutes of a drive. It dries the evaporator and discourages mold.
- **Keep a small bin in the car**, and empty it.
- **Leave the windows cracked** on warm dry days if the car is parked somewhere safe.

## The honest limits

Most smells come out completely. Two are stubborn. Years of cigarette smoke get into the headliner, the vents and even the plastics; we can make a dramatic difference, but sometimes a faint trace remains on hot days. And mildew that has reached the padding under the carpet can mean the carpet has to come out to dry fully, which is a workshop job rather than a driveway one.

We will always tell you before we start what we expect to achieve. A cabin that smells of nothing at all is usually very achievable, and it is a much nicer thing than a cabin that smells of pine.
