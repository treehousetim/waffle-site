Wheels are the dirtiest part of any car, and the most often damaged by cleaning. Most of the dirt is **brake dust**: fine particles of iron and pad material, thrown off red hot every time you brake, which embed in the lacquer of your wheels and slowly pit it.

Here is how we clean wheels on every visit, and how you can do the same between them.

## Wheels first, with their own kit

We always clean the wheels before the paint, for two reasons. Wheel cleaners can splash onto the bodywork, and you want to rinse that off with the main wash. And the brushes, mitts and water that touch your wheels must never touch your paint, because brake dust is abrasive.

So wheels get their own bucket, their own brushes and their own mitt. Ours are a different color from everything else in the van, so they never get mixed up.

## Pick the right cleaner

Most modern alloy wheels are painted and then lacquered, just like your car's bodywork. Some are polished, chrome, powder-coated or bare machined aluminium. That matters, because the wrong cleaner can cloud, stain or strip them.

- **Avoid strong acid wheel cleaners** unless you know exactly what your wheels are made of. They work fast, but they can etch polished and bare aluminium and damage some finishes permanently.
- **Use a pH-balanced or iron-removing wheel cleaner** for regular cleaning. Iron removers react with the iron particles in brake dust and turn purple as they work, which looks dramatic and is perfectly safe on lacquered wheels.
- **Never spray cleaner onto hot wheels.** It dries instantly and leaves marks. Let the car cool for at least half an hour after driving.

## The method

1. **Rinse** each wheel thoroughly to knock off loose dust.
2. **Spray the cleaner** onto the wheel, tire and arch, and let it dwell for the time on the label. Do one wheel at a time so nothing dries.
3. **Agitate** with soft brushes: a large soft brush for the face, a long thin brush for the barrel behind the spokes, a small detailing brush for the lug nuts and the valve. The barrel is where most of the dust collects, and the part most people miss.
4. **Scrub the tire sidewall** with a stiffer brush. Brown, dull tires are usually just dirty; the "blooming" of old dressing and road grime comes off with a tire cleaner.
5. **Rinse thoroughly**, including the arch and the brake caliper.
6. **Dry** the wheel with a dedicated towel or a blower, so water does not spot the face.
7. **Dress the tires** with a water-based, satin dressing. Avoid greasy, high-gloss silicone dressings: they sling off onto your paint as you drive.

## Protect them

The best thing you can do for wheels is to make them easier to clean. A wheel sealant or a ceramic coating on the faces stops brake dust bonding as quickly, so most of it rinses off. ## How often?

Every time you wash the car, ideally every two to four weeks. Left for months, brake dust bakes on and pits the lacquer, and by then no cleaner will make the wheel look new again.
