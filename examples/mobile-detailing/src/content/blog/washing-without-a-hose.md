The question we are asked most often, by far, is some version of: "Do you need my hose?" The answer is no. We do not need your hose, your outlet, your garage or anything else from your house. Here is how that works, and why it makes for a better wash, not just a more convenient one.

## Water we bring

Every Gloss Theory van carries a 120-gallon tank of **deionised water**. Before it goes into the tank, our water is filtered through a column of ion-exchange resin, which removes the dissolved minerals that tap water carries: calcium, magnesium, sodium and the rest.

That matters for one reason above all: **water spots**. When ordinary water dries on paint, it leaves its minerals behind as white rings, and in the sun those can etch into the clear coat. Deionised water has nothing to leave behind. It can dry on the car without marking it, which means we never have to rush the drying or rub hard to beat the sun.

## How little we use

A garden hose left running puts out several gallons a minute. A careful wash does not need anywhere near that.

- **Snow foam** pre-soak: a few gallons of foam mixed from concentrate.
- **Two-bucket wash**: two buckets, about four gallons each.
- **Rinse**: a low-flow, pressure-controlled rinse from the tank.

All told, an Express Wash on an average car uses about **15 gallons**. A full day of six cars uses less water than a garden sprinkler running for an hour.

## Power we bring

The van carries a 3.5 kW inverter generator for the pressure washer, the vacuum, the extractor and the polishers. Inverter generators are much quieter than the old open-frame type: at the curb, ours is quieter than two people talking. We run it on the street side of the van, away from your windows, and we switch it off whenever nothing needs it.

## Runoff we catch

Every storm drain in Port Alder leads somewhere: the bay, the river or Bright Lake. Car shampoo, brake dust and road grime do not belong in any of them, and in Brightwater the homeowners' association rightly forbids it.

So where it matters, we wash the car on a **reclaim mat**: a flat, heavy rubber mat with inflatable edges that you drive onto, or that we lay around the car. Everything that runs off the car stays on the mat. A small pump lifts it into a 60-gallon recovery tank on the van, and we dispose of it properly back at the yard.

On ordinary driveways with no drains nearby, we use a runoff mat at the foot of the drive so our water does not run into the street or onto a neighbor's car.

## What we do need from you

Just space: a parking spot for the van within about a hundred feet of the car (our hose and power reels are a hundred feet long), and room to walk all the way around the car, about three feet on each side. For an interior, the car unlocked or the key. For [paint correction](/packages/paint-correction/) and [ceramic coating](/packages/ceramic-coating/), a garage or covered space, because polish and coatings must stay out of direct sun and rain.
