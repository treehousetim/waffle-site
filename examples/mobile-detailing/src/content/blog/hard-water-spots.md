You wash the car, park it in the sun to dry, and come back to a constellation of white rings on the hood. Or the neighbor's sprinkler catches the side of the car overnight. Either way, you have water spots, and they are more serious than they look.

## What a water spot is

Tap water, well water and even rain carry dissolved minerals: mostly calcium and magnesium, plus whatever the air or the pipes add. When a drop of water evaporates, the water leaves and the minerals stay, as a small ring of dried deposit.

Detailers think about water spots in three stages:

1. **Surface deposits**. Fresh rings sitting on top of the paint. A gentle wash, or a dedicated water-spot remover, takes them off.
2. **Bonded deposits**. Minerals that have hardened on over days or weeks. They need a mild acid cleaner and patience.
3. **Etching**. In strong sun, the mineral ring concentrates and heats up, and eats a shallow crater into the clear coat itself. Etched spots do not wash off at all. They have to be polished out.

Dark cars show it most, but every color suffers. Glass etches too.

## Why some places are worse

The harder the water, the bigger the deposit. In Port Alder, homes on mains water get moderately hard water; lakeside homes in Brightwater that draw from wells often have very hard water. Sprinklers are the worst offenders of all: they wet the same side of a car night after night, and the morning sun bakes it.

## How to prevent them

- **Do not wash in direct sun.** Water evaporates before you can dry it. Early morning, evening or shade.
- **Dry the car properly**, every time. Do not let it air-dry. A blower and a plush microfiber towel are ideal.
- **Use softened or deionised water for the final rinse** if you can. This is why every Gloss Theory van carries deionised water: water with no dissolved minerals cannot leave a deposit, so it can dry on the paint without marking it.
- **Move the car away from sprinklers**, or move the sprinkler.
- **Protect the paint.** A sealant or a ceramic coating makes water bead and roll off instead of sitting in flat puddles, and gives the minerals something sacrificial to sit on instead of your clear coat.

## What to do about spots you already have

Start gently. Wash the car, then try a dedicated water-spot remover on one panel, following its instructions exactly and never letting it dry on the paint. Many fresh spots will disappear.

If the rings are still visible after a wash and a remover, run a fingertip over one. If it feels smooth but you can still see a ring, it is probably etched. Etching needs machine polishing: a light, single-stage [paint correction](/packages/paint-correction/) usually removes it completely, unless it has been left for years.

Do not try to scrub spots off with household vinegar, a magic eraser or a kitchen sponge. Acidic household cleaners are unpredictable on trim, and abrasive pads will leave far worse damage than the spots.

## Coated cars get spots too

A common surprise: a ceramic coating does not make a car immune to water spots. It makes water bead, and a field of small beads drying in the sun can leave a field of small spots, this time on the coating rather than the paint. The fix is the same: dry the car, rinse with soft water, and have the coating decontaminated now and then so minerals do not build up and dull its beading.

The simplest rule: never let water dry on the car by itself. Everything else follows from that.
