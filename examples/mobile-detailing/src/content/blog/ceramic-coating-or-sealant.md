Two cars leave our van on the same afternoon. One has a spray sealant, the other a ceramic coating. On the drive home, in the rain, they look almost identical: deep gloss, water racing off in tight beads. Six months later they look very different. Here is why, and how to decide which one belongs on your car.

## What they have in common

Both are a clear layer that sits on top of your clear coat. Both make the surface slick and **hydrophobic**, so water beads and rolls off taking dirt with it. Both add gloss and make washing easier. And both need clean, decontaminated paint underneath to bond properly, which is why we never apply either to a car that has not been washed, iron-treated and clayed first.

## Sealant: easy, cheap, temporary

A modern synthetic sealant is a polymer that bonds loosely to the paint. It goes on in minutes, wipes off easily and looks superb.

- **Lasts**: about three months on a daily driver, less on a car parked outside by the sea.
- **Hardness**: soft. It adds slickness but almost no resistance to scratching.
- **Cost**: included in our [Full Detail](/packages/full-detail/), and a small add-on to an Express Wash.
- **Forgiving**: if it is applied a little unevenly, the next top-up fixes it.

Sealant is the right answer if you change cars every year or two, if your car lives in a garage, or if you like the ritual of a fresh layer every season.

## Ceramic coating: hard, durable, permanent-ish

A ceramic coating is a liquid that cures into a thin, hard, glass-like layer chemically bonded to the clear coat. Once it has cured, it does not wash off.

- **Lasts**: rated for five years with proper washing. In practice it slowly loses some of its beading over that time, but the protection stays.
- **Hardness**: harder than clear coat. It resists light wash marring, bird-dropping etching and UV fading. It does not stop stone chips or deep scratches: it is microns thick.
- **Cost**: much more, because the paint must be polished first. A coating locks in whatever is underneath, swirls included.
- **Unforgiving**: high spots and streaks have to be levelled within minutes, and the coating must stay dry while it cures. That is why we use inspection lights, infrared curing lamps and ask for a garage.

## The question that decides it

Ask yourself how long you will keep the car, and how much you enjoy washing it.

If the answer is "years" and "not much", a coating pays for itself in time saved: a coated car can often be cleaned with a rinse, a gentle foam and a quick dry. If the answer is "a year or two" or "I like doing it", sealant gives you most of the look for a fraction of the cost.

## What coatings do not do

There is a lot of exaggeration around ceramic coatings, so to be plain:

- A coated car still gets dirty. Dirt just lets go more easily.
- A coated car still needs gentle washing. Brush car washes will eventually mar it.
- A coating will not hide existing swirls. It makes them more visible, if anything, by adding gloss on top.
- A coating does not make paint chip-proof. For that you want paint protection film.

## Looking after either

The care is the same: wash every two to four weeks with a pH-neutral shampoo, two buckets and a soft mitt, and dry gently. Avoid strong degreasers and "wash and wax" products that leave their own film on top. Rinse off bird droppings and tree sap quickly.

Coatings benefit from an occasional decontamination wash to remove iron and mineral deposits that dull the beading. We do one free at three months after every [ceramic coating](/packages/ceramic-coating/) we apply, and it is a good moment to check how the coating is behaving.
