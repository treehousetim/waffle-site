Fleet managers ask us this all the time, and the honest answer is: more often than you think, and less disruptively than you fear. Here is how we think about it for the businesses we look after.

## Why it matters more than it looks

A company van with your name on the side drives past hundreds of potential customers a day. When it is clean, it says you are organised and you take care. When it is filthy, it says the opposite, in large letters.

Beyond appearance, regular washing protects the asset. Road grime, salt and brake dust shorten the life of paint, wheels and seals. Lease companies charge for it at the end of the term. And a vehicle that is cleaned regularly gets looked at regularly: we often spot damage, a soft tire or a failed bulb before anyone else does.

## A starting schedule

Every fleet is different, but these are the rotas we most often set up:

| Vehicle | Exterior wash | Interior | Full detail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Liveried service vans | Every 2 weeks | Monthly, cab only | Twice a year |
| Pool cars | Every 2 to 4 weeks | Monthly | Twice a year |
| Executive or client-facing cars | Weekly or fortnightly | Fortnightly | Twice a year |
| Dealer or display stock | Before each viewing | As needed | On arrival |

In winter, or for vehicles working near the coast or on construction sites, add a quick rinse between washes. Salt and cement dust do most of their damage in the weeks you leave them.

## Scheduling without downtime

The usual objection to fleet washing is not cost but time: a van at the car wash is a van not working. That is exactly the problem a mobile service solves. We come to where the vehicles already are, at the times they would be sitting still anyway:

- **Early mornings** in the yard, before the first shift goes out.
- **During the working day** in an office lot, while staff are at their desks.
- **Evenings** when vans come back in, so they leave clean the next morning.

Each vehicle on an account has its own record: what was done, when, the photos, and anything we noticed. One invoice arrives at the end of the month with every vehicle itemised.

## Staff car days

One of the most popular things we do in Northgate Business Park is an office car day. We park in the lot for a day, staff book their own cars in at a team rate, and they leave their keys at reception. It costs the company nothing but a parking space, and it is a perk people actually use. Several of our [fleet accounts](/fleet/) started that way.

## What a fleet account needs

To set one up, we need to know roughly how many vehicles, what kind, where they park, and when they are usually idle. We visit, look at the vehicles, and send a written rota and price. Most accounts start with a full detail of every vehicle to establish a baseline, then move to a regular wash rota.
