Old Harbor is one of the nicest places in Port Alder to live and one of the hardest places to keep a car looking good. The reason is invisible. Every night the wind comes off the bay, it carries a fine mist of salt water inland, and that mist settles on every car parked within a few blocks of the quays.

## What salt does

Salt on its own is not very aggressive. Salt plus moisture is. Dissolved salt is an electrolyte: it speeds up corrosion wherever bare metal is exposed, and it keeps surfaces damp for longer because it draws moisture out of the air.

On a coastal car, that shows up in a few places:

- **Paint.** A fine, slightly gritty film that dulls the gloss. On its own it does not eat through clear coat, but rubbed off dry it scratches, and it leaves the surface rougher, so dirt sticks faster.
- **Stone chips.** Every chip that reaches bare metal is a place for rust to start, and salt gets it going much faster. Look for orange specks on the hood and the leading edge of the roof.
- **Wheels and brakes.** Salt combines with brake dust into a crust that pits alloy wheels and seizes caliper hardware.
- **The underside.** Sills, wheel arches, suspension parts and the exhaust collect salt where you never see it and never wash it.
- **Glass and seals.** Salt haze on glass is harder to cut through than ordinary dirt, and rubber seals dry out faster.

## A routine for coastal cars

**Rinse more than you wash.** The single most useful thing you can do is a gentle rinse every week or two, especially after a windy spell. Fresh water dissolves salt and carries it away before it builds up. Never wipe a salty car dry: rinse first, always.

**Wash properly every two to three weeks.** Snow foam, two buckets, a soft mitt, and dry with a blower and a plush towel. On every Old Harbor visit, we also do a low-pressure rinse of the wheel arches and sills, because that is where salt sits longest.

**Protect the paint, and top it up more often.** Inland, a spray sealant lasts about three months. By the water, we suggest topping it up every two to three. A [ceramic coating](/packages/ceramic-coating/) is an even better barrier for coastal cars: it is harder than clear coat, it does not wash off, and salt film rinses off it far more easily.

**Decontaminate twice a year.** Iron fallout remover and a clay bar take out the bonded grit and rail dust that make paint feel rough. A [Full Detail](/packages/full-detail/) includes both.

**Touch up chips quickly.** A small pot of touch-up paint from the dealer, applied with a fine brush, stops a chip turning into a rust spot. If you are unsure how, ask us to show you at the end of a visit.

**Look after the wheels.** Clean them with a pH-balanced wheel cleaner rather than strong acids, and consider coating the wheel faces: brake dust and salt crust release far more easily from a coated wheel.

## Garages help, but are not enough

A garage keeps the night mist off, which makes a real difference. But the car still drives along the waterfront, still parks by the quays at lunch, and still carries salt home in its wheel arches. A garaged car by the sea needs less care than one parked on the street, but it still needs more than one inland.
