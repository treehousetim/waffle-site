Of everything that lands on a car, two things do damage faster than any other: bird droppings and tree sap. Both are worst in summer, both are acidic or sticky enough to bond to paint, and both can leave a permanent mark in a matter of days if the sun gets to work on them.

The good news is that both come off easily if you act quickly and gently.

## Why bird droppings are so harmful

Bird droppings are acidic, and they often contain grit from seeds and stones. On a hot panel, the clear coat softens and expands slightly in the heat; the dropping dries and contracts on top of it; when the paint cools, it sets around the shape of the deposit. That leaves a dull, slightly crinkled mark called **etching**, even after the dropping itself is gone.

On a dark car in full summer sun, that can happen in a day.

### How to remove one

1. **Soften it first.** Lay a microfiber cloth soaked in warm water (or a quick detailer spray) over the dropping for a minute or two.
2. **Lift, do not scrub.** Pinch the softened deposit up and away with the cloth, folding it in on itself. Wiping across the paint drags the grit with it.
3. **Repeat if needed**, with a clean side of the cloth each time.
4. **Rinse the area**, and re-apply a little sealant or quick detailer if you have one.

Keep a small spray bottle and two clean microfiber cloths in the trunk through the summer. It is the cheapest paint protection there is.

## Tree sap

In Port Alder, the trees that cause most trouble are the old lindens along the streets of Cinder Hill. From late June into August, aphids feeding in the canopy drip a sticky substance called honeydew onto everything below. It is technically not sap, but it behaves the same way: it lands as fine sticky specks, collects dust, and in the heat hardens into small amber beads that are bonded to the paint.

Conifers and maples drop true sap, which can be thicker and harder.

### How to remove it

- **Fresh specks** often wash off with a good shampoo and a little patience.
- **Hardened beads** need a solvent. A dedicated sap or tar remover, or isopropyl alcohol diluted with water, applied to a cloth and held on the spot for thirty seconds, softens them so they lift away.
- **Never pick or scrape** with a fingernail, a blade or a scourer. You will take clear coat with it.
- **Follow up** with a wash of the area and a fresh layer of protection, because solvents strip wax and sealant.

We carry sap remover on every Cinder Hill visit through the summer, and we work bead by bead rather than scrubbing.

## When the mark stays behind

If you have removed the deposit and a dull outline remains, the clear coat is etched. Light etching usually polishes out with a single-stage machine polish; deeper etching may need more. That is a job for [paint correction](/packages/paint-correction/), and it is worth doing before the next summer adds to it.

## Prevention

- **Park out from under trees** in early summer if you can, especially lindens.
- **Keep the paint protected.** A sealant or [ceramic coating](/packages/ceramic-coating/) does not make droppings harmless, but it gives you a sacrificial layer and much more time to clean up before etching starts.
- **Use a car cover** for a car that sits for long periods under trees.
- **Clean within the day** in summer. In winter, a few days is usually fine.

The rule is simple: the sooner it comes off, the less it costs.
