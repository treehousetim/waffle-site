Clear aligners are a series of thin, clear plastic trays, each very slightly different, that move teeth a small step at a time. They work well for many adults and teenagers with crowding, gaps or mild bite problems. They are not magic, they are not right for everyone, and they only work if you wear them. Here is what I tell people at a first consultation.

## How they move teeth

Each set of aligners is shaped for where your teeth will be next, not where they are now. Worn most of the day, the gentle, steady pressure lets the bone around each tooth remodel, and the tooth moves. After a week or two you move on to the next set.

Sometimes we bond small tooth-coloured bumps, called attachments, onto a few teeth. They give the aligners something to grip for movements that need a bit more help. They come off at the end and the tooth is polished smooth.

## What wearing them is really like

- **You wear them about 22 hours a day.** They come out to eat, to drink anything other than water, and to brush. That is the deal, and it is the single biggest thing that decides how well treatment goes.
- **New trays feel tight.** For a day or two after each change, most people feel pressure and some tenderness. It fades quickly.
- **You might lisp, briefly.** Most people sound normal again within a few days.
- **You brush more.** After eating, brush or at least rinse before the trays go back in, or food gets trapped against your teeth.
- **You will lose the case.** Everybody does once. Aligners wrapped in a napkin end up in the bin far more often than you would think.

## How long does it take?

It depends entirely on how much movement your teeth need. Small corrections can take a few months; more complex cases take longer. I give an estimate at the consultation once I have seen your scan, and we check progress every six to ten weeks.

## Retainers are not optional

Teeth like to drift back to where they were. When the last aligner is done, you wear a retainer, usually at night, for the long term. Skip it and some of your hard work will slowly undo itself. I would rather tell you that now than surprise you later.

## When aligners are not the answer

Some bites, some jaw problems and some very large movements are better treated another way, sometimes by an orthodontic specialist. And sometimes the honest answer is that your teeth are healthy, your bite works, and the thing that bothers you is small enough to leave alone or fix in a much simpler way, like reshaping one edge.

A good share of my consultations end with “honestly, your teeth are fine”. I am proud of that.

## The consultation

We start with a check-up, then a 3D scan: a small wand that photographs your teeth in a few minutes, with no putty. You see a simulation of how your teeth might move, we talk about what bothers you, and you go home to think about it. There is no obligation to go ahead.
