Gums that bleed when you brush or floss are usually inflamed, and the most common cause is plaque sitting along the gumline. The good news is that this early stage, called gingivitis, is very common and usually improves with better cleaning at home and a professional clean. The instinct to brush more gently, or stop flossing, because it bleeds is understandable, but it is usually the opposite of what helps.

I am one of the hygienists at Brightside, and this is probably the conversation I have most often.

## Why gums bleed

Plaque is a soft, sticky film of bacteria that builds up on teeth every day. Left along the gumline, it irritates the gums, which become red, puffy and quick to bleed. Healthy gums are pale pink, firm, and do not bleed when brushed.

If plaque stays long enough, it hardens into tartar, which a toothbrush cannot remove. Tartar keeps the irritation going, which is why a professional clean matters even for good brushers.

Other things can make gums more likely to bleed, including pregnancy, some medicines, smoking, diabetes and simply brushing too hard. If you are worried about any of these, mention them at your visit.

## Keep going, gently

When gums bleed, people tend to avoid them. That leaves more plaque behind, the gums get more inflamed, and the bleeding continues. Instead:

1. **Brush twice a day** with a soft brush, angled towards the gumline, using small gentle circles.
2. **Clean between your teeth once a day.** Floss, small interdental brushes or a water flosser. A little bleeding at first is common.
3. **Give it two weeks.** With gentle, regular cleaning, inflamed gums often start to settle. If the bleeding is not improving, book a visit.

Our post on [how to brush properly](/blog/how-to-brush-properly/) goes into technique in more detail.

## Why it is worth taking seriously

Gingivitis can usually be turned around. Left alone for a long time, though, inflammation can spread below the gumline and affect the bone that holds teeth in place, which is called periodontitis. That stage is harder to treat and can, over the years, lead to loose teeth.

Signs that need checking promptly include gums that have pulled back from the teeth, teeth that feel loose or have shifted, persistent bad breath, or pus around the gums.

## What happens at a cleaning

At your appointment I measure your gums gently in a few places to see how healthy they are, then remove plaque and tartar above and just below the gumline, with fine hand tools or an ultrasonic scaler that uses vibration and water. If your gums are tender, tell me: we can use numbing gel, go slower, or split the clean over two visits.

Then we talk about your routine. No lectures, I promise. Just one or two small changes that suit your mouth and your life.

If your gums have been bleeding for a while, book a [check-up and cleaning](/treatments/checkups-and-cleanings/). Healthy gums are one of the most satisfying things to see come back.
