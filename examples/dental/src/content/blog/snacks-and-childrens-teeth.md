For children’s teeth, how often they have sugary or acidic food and drink matters more than how much. Every snack or sip starts a short spell when the bacteria on teeth make acid. Teeth can recover between those spells, but not if they never get a break. So the single most useful change is often to keep sweet things to mealtimes and give teeth time off in between.

I see a lot of worried parents, and I want to say first: you do not need to ban birthday cake. Here is a practical version.

## Why grazing is hard on teeth

After sugar, the bacteria in plaque produce acid for a while, and that acid softens enamel. Saliva slowly neutralises it and helps the enamel recover. A biscuit with lunch is one acid spell. The same biscuit nibbled over an hour, or a juice sipped all afternoon, keeps teeth under attack for much longer.

## Snacks that are kinder to teeth

- cheese, plain yoghurt, and milk;
- vegetable sticks, such as carrot, cucumber and pepper;
- crackers, breadsticks, plain popcorn for older children;
- fruit is fine, and better whole than as juice or dried fruit, which sticks to teeth.

Sticky sweets, dried fruit snacks and sweetened cereal bars are the ones we see causing the most trouble, because they cling to teeth for a long time.

## Drinks

- **Water and milk** are the best everyday drinks.
- **Juice and smoothies**, if you give them, are better with a meal, in a cup rather than sipped from a bottle, and in small amounts.
- **Fizzy drinks and sports drinks** are acidic as well as sugary, even the sugar-free ones, which can still soften enamel.

## Bottles and bedtime

Try not to put a baby or toddler to bed with a bottle of milk or juice. While they sleep, saliva flow drops and the drink pools around the teeth. After the last feed and teeth brushing, only water.

By around their first birthday, start moving from bottles to an open or free-flowing cup.

## Brushing, as part of the plan

- Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste: a smear for under-threes, a pea-sized amount after that.
- Brush last thing at night, and nothing but water after.
- Help them brush until they are about seven or eight.
- Spit, don’t rinse: the fluoride left behind keeps working.

## Treats, without the guilt

Treats are part of childhood. Have them with a meal, or as a single after-dinner treat rather than spread across the day, and follow with a drink of water. That alone makes a real difference.
