Brush twice a day for two minutes with a fluoride toothpaste, angle the bristles towards the gumline, use gentle little circles rather than scrubbing, and spit, don’t rinse. That covers most of it. But since almost everyone we see has one habit worth tweaking, here is the detail.

## Choosing a toothbrush

- **Soft or medium bristles.** Hard bristles and hard scrubbing can wear the gums back over time. Firm is not the same as thorough.
- **A small head** reaches the back teeth more easily.
- **Manual or electric?** Both work well when used properly. Many people find an electric brush makes good technique easier, especially with a timer. If you have a manual brush you like, keep it.
- **Replace it** every three months or so, or sooner if the bristles splay. A splayed brush cleans poorly.

## How much toothpaste

For adults and children from about three, a pea-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste. For children under three, a smear the size of a grain of rice. More foam does not mean cleaner teeth.

## The technique

1. **Angle the brush** at about 45 degrees towards the gumline. Plaque collects where the tooth meets the gum, and that is the spot most of us miss.
2. **Small, gentle circles**, or short back-and-forth strokes, a tooth or two at a time.
3. **Go in an order.** Outside surfaces, inside surfaces, then chewing surfaces, top and bottom. An order means nothing gets skipped.
4. **Don’t forget the insides** of the bottom front teeth, where tartar likes to build up, and the backs of your last molars.
5. **Two minutes.** It feels longer than it is. A timer or a song helps.

## Spit, don’t rinse

This is the habit most people are surprised by. Rinsing with water straight after brushing washes away the fluoride that keeps working on your teeth. Spit out the extra toothpaste and leave it at that. If you use a mouthwash, use it at a different time of day.

## Between your teeth

A toothbrush cannot reach between teeth. Floss, interdental brushes or a water flosser all work: the best one is the one you will actually use, once a day. If your gums bleed when you start, keep going gently. It usually settles within a week or two as they get healthier. If it does not, mention it at your next visit. Our post on [bleeding gums](/blog/bleeding-gums/) explains why.

## When to brush

Last thing at night, and one other time during the day. Brushing at night matters most, because saliva flow drops while you sleep. If you have had something acidic, like fruit juice, wait half an hour before brushing.

## Children

Help children brush until they are about seven or eight: they do not have the coordination to do it thoroughly before then. Standing behind them and tilting their head back makes it much easier to see what you are doing.
