For most adults with healthy teeth and gums, whitening done under a dentist’s supervision is considered safe. The usual side effect is some temporary sensitivity, which fades. The problems we see almost always come from whitening that skipped the first step: making sure the teeth were healthy enough to whiten in the first place.

## What whitening does

Whitening gels use a peroxide that soaks into the tooth and breaks down the molecules that cause discolouration. It does not scrub or sand the surface away. That is why professional whitening can change the colour of the tooth itself, not just remove surface stains the way a polish does.

It also explains its limits. Whitening only works on natural enamel. Fillings, crowns, bonding and veneers stay exactly the colour they were, which is why we always look at your mouth first: a bright tooth next to an old filling can look worse than before.

## Why teeth can feel sensitive

During whitening, your teeth can become temporarily more sensitive to cold. Most people feel it as a brief zing when they drink something chilled. It normally settles within a day or two of finishing or taking a break.

Things that help:

- brushing with a sensitive toothpaste for a couple of weeks before and during whitening;
- wearing trays for a shorter time each day, or every other day;
- avoiding very cold drinks while you are whitening.

If sensitivity is strong or does not settle, stop and call us. That is part of what a supervised treatment is for.

## Who it does not suit

Whitening is usually not recommended:

- during pregnancy or breastfeeding;
- for children and young teenagers whose teeth are still developing;
- over untreated decay, cracked teeth or gum disease;
- where the discolouration comes from a tooth that has been damaged or has had a root canal, which needs a different approach.

None of these are reasons to be disappointed. They are reasons for a conversation, and often there is a better option.

## Shop kits and salons

Over-the-counter kits use one-size trays that let gel leak onto your gums, which is uncomfortable and can sting for days. They also cannot tell whether your yellow tooth is a stain, a dark filling showing through, or a tooth that needs attention. And “whitening” offered by people who are not dental professionals is not always what it claims to be.

## How we do it

First, a check-up if you are due. Then we note your starting shade, take a quick 3D scan for trays that fit your teeth exactly, and choose a gel strength for you. Most people whiten at home for an hour or so a day for a couple of weeks; some prefer one longer visit in the chair.

I will not promise you a shade. Teeth respond differently, and honest expectations make for happier smiles. What I will do is show you your starting point, tell you what I would expect for your teeth, and keep an eye on you as you go.
