If the thought of a dental appointment makes your stomach turn, you are far from alone, and there are lots of small, practical things that make it easier. None of them needs you to be braver. They are about putting you back in control, which is what dental anxiety mostly takes away.

I was a very nervous patient myself, so this list comes from both sides of the chair.

## Before the day

- **Tell us.** Say “I get nervous” when you book. It changes how we plan everything: a longer slot, a quieter time, and a phone call beforehand so you know a friendly voice.
- **Choose your time.** First thing in the morning is popular with anxious patients: there is no day of dread beforehand. Others like the hush of a late appointment.
- **Ask for a talk-only visit.** Your first appointment can be twenty minutes and a cup of tea in a normal room, with no treatment at all. Meeting the person is often half the battle.
- **Write your worries down.** Needles, the noise, gagging, being judged: whatever it is, tell us. Every fear has something we can do about it.

## On the day

- **Eat something light** beforehand, and skip the third coffee: caffeine makes nerves jumpier.
- **Bring headphones.** Music or a podcast covers the sounds that bother many people. We have spares.
- **Bring someone.** A friend or partner can sit beside you the whole time.
- **Arrive just on time.** Sitting in a waiting room winding yourself up does not help. We run on time so you will not wait long.

## In the chair

- **Agree a stop signal.** At Brightside it is raising your hand, and it always works: we stop, every time. Some people like a second signal for “slow down”.
- **Ask for a running commentary**, or ask for silence. Some people want to know everything as it happens; others would rather not. Both are fine.
- **Sit up between steps.** Lying back can feel vulnerable. We can do a lot with the chair more upright, and sit you up to talk whenever you like.
- **Breathe slowly.** In through your nose for four, out through your mouth for four. It sounds too simple to work, but slow breathing really does settle a racing heart. We practise it with patients before we start.
- **Use the comfort kit.** Every room has a blanket, sunglasses for the bright light, and something to squeeze.

## About the injection

For many people the injection is the worst part. We use a numbing gel first, wait for it to work, and go slowly. Most patients describe a small pinch or pressure rather than a sting. Tell us if you feel anything and we pause.

## Be kind to yourself

Dental anxiety usually comes from a bad experience, often in childhood. It is not a weakness and it is not your fault. Every visit that goes well, even a tiny one, makes the next one easier.
