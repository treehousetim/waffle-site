Sensitive teeth usually mean that the softer layer under your enamel, called dentine, is a little exposed. Dentine has tiny channels that lead towards the nerve, so cold, sweet or acidic things set off a short, sharp twinge. It is very common, often easy to improve, and sometimes a sign of something worth checking. Here is how to tell which.

## The usual suspects

- **Gums that have pulled back.** As gums recede, the root surface, which has no enamel, is exposed. Brushing too hard is a common cause, and so is gum disease.
- **Worn enamel.** Acidic food and drink, such as fizzy drinks, fruit juice and sports drinks, soften enamel over time, especially when sipped all day.
- **Grinding or clenching.** Many people do it at night without knowing. It wears enamel and can make teeth ache in the morning.
- **A crack, a worn filling or decay.** Sensitivity in one tooth, especially when you bite, is worth having checked.
- **After whitening or a filling.** Short-lived sensitivity is normal and usually settles within days or a few weeks.

## Things to try at home

1. **Switch to a sensitive toothpaste** and use it for at least a couple of weeks: they take time to work. A trick that helps: last thing at night, rub a little onto the sensitive spot with a clean finger and do not rinse.
2. **Brush gently** with a soft brush, angled at the gumline. Scrubbing hard makes things worse.
3. **Have acidic drinks with meals**, not sipped through the day, and drink water afterwards.
4. **Wait before brushing** for about half an hour after something acidic, so softened enamel is not brushed away.
5. **Use a straw** for cold or acidic drinks, if it helps.

## When to come in

Book a visit if:

- the sensitivity is in one particular tooth;
- it hurts when you bite down;
- the pain lingers for more than a few seconds after the cold has gone;
- it wakes you at night, or throbs on its own;
- a sensitive toothpaste has not helped after a few weeks.

Lingering or throbbing pain is different from a quick zing, and it can mean the nerve inside the tooth is irritated. That is worth seeing sooner rather than later. If it is severe, call us: we keep same-day appointments for [emergencies](/treatments/emergency-dental-care/).

## What we can do

At a check-up we look for the cause first: a receding gum, a worn spot, a crack, or signs of grinding. Depending on what we find, options might include a protective varnish painted onto sensitive areas, sealing an exposed root surface, replacing a worn filling, or a night guard if you grind. Sometimes it is simply a change of brush and technique.

We will always explain what we see and what the choices are, and there is no rush to decide.

Sensitive teeth are one of the most common things people mention at a [check-up](/treatments/checkups-and-cleanings/). If yours are bothering you, mention it next time, or book in sooner.
