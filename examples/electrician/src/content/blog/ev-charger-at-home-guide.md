Most electric cars are charged at home, overnight. Getting the setup right once saves years of small frustrations.

## Level 1 or Level 2?

A **Level 1** cord in a regular outlet adds 3 to 4 miles of range an hour. That covers a short commute and not much else.

A **Level 2** charger on a 240-volt circuit adds 25 to 40 miles an hour. Almost every EV owner who drives daily ends up wanting one.

## How big a circuit?

Most chargers run on 40 or 60-amp circuits, delivering 32 or 48 amps. More is faster, but for overnight charging 32 amps fills almost any car. If your panel is tight, a smaller circuit is a perfectly good answer.

## Hardwired or plug-in?

Hardwired chargers can deliver the most power and avoid the wear that comes from an outlet being used every day. Plug-in chargers on a 14-50 outlet are easy to take with you when you move. Either is fine when installed properly.

## Before you call

- Take a photo of your panel with the door open.
- Decide where the car parks and which side the charge port is on.
- Check your utility for EV rebates and off-peak rates.
