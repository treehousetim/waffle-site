For a few years in the late 1960s and 1970s, copper was expensive and many homes were wired with aluminum branch circuits.

## Why it is a concern

Aluminum expands and contracts more than copper as it heats and cools, and it oxidizes at connections. Over decades, connections at outlets and switches can loosen and overheat.

## How to tell

Look at the cable in the basement or attic: the jacket is often printed with "AL" or "ALUMINUM". Inside a switch or outlet box, the wire is dull silver rather than copper colored.

## Making it safe

You do not always need a full rewire. The recognised repair is to join a short copper pigtail to each aluminum wire with a connector rated for the job, at every outlet, switch and fixture. Done properly, it addresses the connections where the trouble starts.
