A GFCI (ground-fault circuit interrupter) compares the current going out on the hot wire with the current coming back on the neutral. If they differ by more than a few thousandths of an amp, the difference is going somewhere it should not, possibly through a person, and the GFCI cuts power in about a fortieth of a second.

## Where they are required

Current code calls for GFCI protection in bathrooms, kitchens (countertop outlets), laundry areas, garages, unfinished basements, crawl spaces, outdoors, and near sinks, tubs and pools. Older homes are not required to add them, but it is one of the cheapest safety upgrades there is.

## Why one outlet controls several

A GFCI outlet protects every outlet wired after it on the same circuit. That is why a dead bathroom outlet is often fixed by pressing reset on a GFCI in another bathroom, or in the garage.

## Test them monthly

Press **test**: the outlet should go dead. Press **reset** to restore it. If it does not trip, or will not reset, replace it.

## When a GFCI keeps tripping

Moisture in an outdoor box, a failing appliance, or a long run of old wiring are the usual causes. If you cannot find it, [we can](/services/repairs/).
