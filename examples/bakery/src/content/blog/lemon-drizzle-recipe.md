This is the lemon drizzle cake that sits on the counter every day, and the one cake on the board that never changes. We tried to retire it once, in favor of something more seasonal, and lasted four days before the neighbors made us bring it back. Here it is, for one loaf tin.

## What makes it

Three things. A lot of lemon zest in the batter, rubbed into the sugar so its oil flavors everything. A thin, sharp syrup poured over while the cake is still hot, so it soaks right through. And a second layer of sugar and juice that sets into a crisp, crackling crust as it cools. That last part is the bit people come back for.

## Ingredients

For a 2-pound loaf tin (about 9 by 5 inches):

**The cake**

- 225 g caster or superfine sugar
- Zest of 3 unwaxed lemons
- 225 g soft unsalted butter
- 4 large eggs, at room temperature
- 225 g self-raising flour (or 225 g all-purpose flour with 2½ teaspoons baking powder)
- A pinch of salt
- 2 tablespoons whole milk

**The drizzle**

- Juice of 2 lemons (about 80 ml)
- 60 g caster sugar, for the syrup
- 100 g granulated sugar, for the crust

## Method

1. **Heat the oven** to 340°F and line the tin with parchment, leaving some overhanging so you can lift the cake out.
2. **Rub the zest into the sugar** with your fingertips for a minute, until the sugar is damp and smells strongly of lemon.
3. **Cream the butter and lemon sugar** until pale and fluffy, at least three minutes with a mixer. Do not rush this: it is where the lightness comes from.
4. **Beat in the eggs** one at a time, adding a spoonful of the flour with the third and fourth if it looks like curdling.
5. **Fold in the flour, salt and milk** with a spatula, just until there are no streaks. Overmixing makes a tough cake.
6. **Bake for 50 to 55 minutes**, until golden, risen with a crack along the top, and a skewer comes out clean. If it browns too fast, lay a sheet of foil over it for the last 15 minutes.

## The drizzle

While the cake bakes, warm the lemon juice and the 60 g of caster sugar in a small pan just until the sugar dissolves. Do not boil it.

As soon as the cake comes out, while it is still in the tin, prick it all over with a skewer, right to the bottom, about forty times. Spoon the warm syrup over slowly, letting it soak in before you add more.

Then, for the crust: stir the granulated sugar into whatever syrup is left (or a squeeze of fresh juice if there is none), and spoon that grainy mixture over the top. It looks wrong. Trust it. As the cake cools, it sets into a sugary crust that crackles when you cut it.

Leave it in the tin until completely cool, at least two hours, before lifting it out.

## Keeping it

Wrapped, it keeps for four days at room temperature, and gets stickier and better on day two. It freezes well in slices.

## Variations we have tried

- **Lime and coconut:** lime instead of lemon, and 50 g of desiccated coconut instead of 50 g of the flour.
- **Blood orange:** in February, when they are around. Softer, and a beautiful color.
- **Lemon and poppy seed:** two tablespoons of poppy seeds folded in with the flour.
