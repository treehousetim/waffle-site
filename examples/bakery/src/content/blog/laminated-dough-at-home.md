Croissants are not difficult. They are slow, and they are fussy about temperature, and those two things together feel like difficulty. Here is how I would make them at home, with what I have learned from laminating about four thousand of them a week.

## Butter is everything

Laminated dough is layers of butter trapped between layers of dough. When it bakes, the water in the butter turns to steam and pushes the layers apart. So the butter matters more than anything else in the recipe.

Buy a European-style or cultured butter with at least 82% fat. It has less water than ordinary butter, so it stays pliable when cold instead of cracking, and it tastes far better. We use a cultured butter from a dairy in the state; any good high-fat butter works.

## Temperature is the whole game

The dough and the butter need to be the same firmness: cold, but bendable. Around 55°F for both is ideal. If the butter is too cold it shatters into pieces inside the dough. Too warm, it melts into the dough and you get bread, not pastry.

You do not need a thermometer. Press the butter block: it should bend without cracking, like modeling clay. Press the dough next to it: it should feel the same. If either is off, back into the fridge for 15 minutes. **Most failed croissants are impatient croissants.**

## The dough (for 12 croissants)

- 500 g bread flour
- 55 g sugar
- 11 g salt
- 10 g instant yeast
- 140 g cold water
- 140 g cold whole milk
- 40 g soft butter
- 280 g cold cultured butter, for the block

Mix everything but the butter block into a smooth, fairly stiff dough, about five minutes by hand. Flatten it into a rectangle, wrap it and refrigerate overnight. At the bakery we add a little levain as well, for flavor; at home the yeast does fine.

## The butter block

Put the cold butter between two sheets of parchment and beat it flat with a rolling pin, then roll it into a neat 7-inch square. Square corners matter: they become the edges of your layers. Chill it until it is the same firmness as the dough.

## Three folds

1. **Lock in.** Roll the dough into a square about 10 inches across, put the butter on it at 45 degrees like a diamond, and fold the corners of the dough over to meet in the middle. Pinch the seams.
2. **First fold.** Roll it out into a long rectangle, about 8 by 24 inches, rolling in one direction only. Fold it in three, like a letter. Wrap and chill 30 minutes.
3. **Second and third folds.** Turn it 90 degrees and do it twice more, chilling between each.

Three letter folds give you 27 layers of butter. More folds is not better: the layers get so thin they merge, and the croissant loses its honeycomb.

## Shape, proof, bake

Roll the dough to about a quarter of an inch thick and cut long triangles, about 4 inches at the base. Stretch each one gently, then roll it up from the base without pressing. Set them on lined trays with the tip underneath.

Proof them somewhere warm but not hot, under 80°F, or the butter melts out. They are ready when they have roughly doubled and wobble like jelly when you shake the tray: two to three hours. Brush with egg beaten with a splash of milk, and bake at 400°F for 18 to 22 minutes until deep golden, darker than you think.

## If it goes wrong

- **Butter leaking in the oven:** they were under-proofed, or proofed too warm.
- **Bready inside, no layers:** the butter got warm during folding and melted in.
- **Butter in chunks:** the butter was too cold and broke. Let it soften a little longer next time.
