I answer every cake order that comes in, and most of the questions are the same handful. So here are the answers, in the order I would ask them if you came to the counter.

## How many people, really?

Start here, because it decides the size and most of the price. Count the people who will actually eat cake, not the guest list: at a wedding with dessert, a third of the room will skip it; at a child’s birthday, every child will have some and most adults will not.

As a rough guide, our [sizes](/cakes/) are cut as generous party slices, about an inch wide:

- **6-inch, two layers:** 8 to 10 people.
- **8-inch, three layers:** 14 to 18 people. Our most ordered size.
- **10-inch, three layers:** 24 to 30.
- **Two tiers:** 40 to 50. **Three tiers:** 70 to 90.

For a wedding, cut smaller and you can feed more from the same cake. If you are between sizes, go up: leftover cake has never upset anybody.

## How far ahead?

At least **five days** for a single-tier cake, because we bake to a schedule and the oven is full of bread by 6am. **Three weeks** for two tiers and **six weeks** for three, both with a tasting. Summer Saturdays book up early, so for a June wedding, write in spring.

If you need something sooner, call us. Sometimes there is room, and there is always a lemon drizzle on the counter.

## Choosing a flavor

Our [cake menu](/cakes/) has seven flavors. Some notes from the bench:

- **Chocolate and salted caramel** is the safe choice for a crowd of mixed ages. Nobody has ever sent it back.
- **Lemon and elderflower** is the one for people who think they do not like cake.
- **Brown butter and pear** is my favorite, and only from October to January, when the pears are good.
- **Fig, honey and pistachio** is for weddings in late summer. It contains nuts.
- **The chocolate olive oil cake** is vegan, and most people cannot tell. It is our answer for a table with dietary needs, because one cake everyone can eat is better than two.

If you want two flavors in a tiered cake, you can choose one per tier.

## What to ask for, and what to skip

**Ask for:** fresh flowers or fruit in season, a simple palette, piped words, a cake topper you bring yourself. We love seasonal fruit and real flowers (we check every one is safe to touch food).

**Skip:** fondant. We do not make fondant-covered cakes, because it tastes of very little and hides the cake. We make semi-naked and buttercream finishes, smooth or textured. We also do not copy other bakers’ cakes from photos. Bring the photo anyway; it tells me a great deal about the mood you want, and I will make you our version of it.

## Allergies

Tell me in the order form, in plain words. We can make cakes without nuts, dairy or eggs, but our kitchen uses all of them on the same benches, along with wheat. For a severe allergy, we will talk it through with you before we take the order, and sometimes the honest answer is that we are not the right kitchen.

## Words on the cake

Up to about forty letters, piped in buttercream. Check the spelling of names, then check it again. I will read your message back to you when I confirm.

## Pickup and delivery

Most cakes are collected from the counter in the morning or around lunchtime. Bring a flat space in the car; the passenger footwell is better than a seat. For weddings and events within 15 miles, we deliver and set up.

## Deposits

When I confirm your order by email, I will ask for a deposit to hold the date: a quarter of the price for single cakes, half for tiers. The rest is due on pickup or delivery.

That is it. When you are ready, [send me your order](/cakes/order/), and I will reply within a working day.
