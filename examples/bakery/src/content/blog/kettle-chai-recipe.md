The café is called Crumb & Kettle because of this pot. Hattie made chai on the stove for the bakers at four in the morning, and the first customers smelled it and asked for some. That was 2014. Now I make a big pot every morning before we open, and this is how, scaled down to two mugs.

## Why whole spices

You can make chai from a powder or a concentrate, and it will taste of sugar and cinnamon. Whole spices, cracked and simmered, give you something layered and warm: sweet cardamom first, then cinnamon and ginger, then a pepper heat at the back of your throat. They also hold their flavor in the jar for months, where ground spices fade in weeks.

## For two mugs

- 6 green cardamom pods
- 1 small stick of cinnamon bark (about 2 inches)
- 4 cloves
- 6 black peppercorns
- A thumb of fresh ginger, sliced
- 350 ml water
- 2 heaped teaspoons strong loose black tea (an Assam is ideal)
- 300 ml whole milk, or oat milk
- Sugar or honey, to taste

## Method

1. **Crack the spices.** Put the cardamom, cinnamon, cloves and peppercorns in a mortar, or in a bag under a rolling pin, and bash them until the cardamom pods split. You want them broken, not powdered.
2. **Toast them.** Warm a small pan over medium heat, add the spices and stir for about a minute, until you can smell them.
3. **Simmer with water.** Add the water and ginger, bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer for 10 minutes. The water will turn the color of weak tea. This is where most of the flavor comes from, so do not cut it short.
4. **Add the tea.** Stir in the tea and simmer 2 minutes more. Strong tea stands up to the milk; a delicate tea disappears.
5. **Add the milk.** Pour in the milk and bring it back up until it just starts to rise up the sides of the pan. Take it off the heat as soon as it does, or it boils over. That rise is the moment.
6. **Strain and sweeten.** Pour through a fine strainer into mugs, and sweeten to taste. Chai is traditionally sweet, and the sugar brings the spices out. We serve ours unsweetened, with honey on the side.

## Make it your own

- **More heat:** double the ginger and add a few more peppercorns.
- **Fennel or star anise:** half a teaspoon of fennel seeds or one star anise for a licorice note.
- **Vegan:** oat milk works best; it does not split when it boils, and it is a little sweet on its own.
- **Iced:** make it double strength with half the milk, cool it, and pour it over ice with cold milk.

## A batch for the week

To keep chai in the fridge, simmer a bigger batch of spices, ginger and tea in water only (step 1 to 4), strain it and chill. It keeps four days. Then heat equal parts of the concentrate and milk for each mug, whenever you want one.
