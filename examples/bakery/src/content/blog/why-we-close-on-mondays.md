Every few weeks someone tries the door on a Monday and leaves a note. The notes are lovely and occasionally rude. So here is why we close on Mondays, and why we are going to keep doing it.

## Bread runs on a two-day clock

A loaf you buy on Tuesday morning was mixed on Monday afternoon. Our country loaves spend a night in the walk-in cooler, our croissant dough three days. So "closed" does not quite mean closed: on Monday afternoon, two of us come in to mix Tuesday’s dough, feed the starter and laminate the croissant dough that will be baked on Wednesday.

What Monday gives us is a morning without the oven. The deck oven runs from four every morning from Tuesday to Sunday. Monday is when it cools down, gets cleaned and gets looked at. Bread ovens are serviced the way cars are, and an oven that fails at five on a Saturday is a very bad day for everyone.

## People run on sleep

Baking is an early job. Most of us are up at three-thirty, six days a week. A bakery that opens seven days needs a second team, and a second team means bread made by people who do not know the starter, or the oven, or Mrs. Ellery’s standing order for two rye loaves.

We would rather be five people who make everything ourselves and have one day off together than be open every day and lose the reason anyone comes in. Monday is the one day all five of us sleep past four.

## What we do on Mondays

Honestly? Sleep, mostly. But also:

- **Taste.** Monday afternoon is when we try new things. The pumpkin and sage danish was a Monday experiment, and so, less successfully, was a beet sourdough.
- **Visit.** Twice a month, June and I drive out to the mill and to the farms we buy from. You learn more about flour in an hour at a mill than in a year of reading the bag.
- **Fix things.** The mixer, the proofer, the chair with the wobbly leg in the window.
- **Eat other people’s bread.** We are customers too, and every baker learns from someone else’s loaf.

## Planning around us

If you need bread for Monday, buy it on Sunday morning: a country loaf keeps well for three days, and [our notes on keeping bread fresh](/journal/keeping-bread-fresh/) will get you through. Cafés we bake for get a double delivery on Saturdays for the same reason, and our [wholesale](/wholesale/) customers can order Monday bread with their Saturday order.

Cake orders can be picked up any day we are open. For a party on a Monday, collect on Sunday: a layer cake is happy in the fridge for a day, and some are better for it.

## Thank you

For understanding, and for the notes. We read them all, including the rude ones. See you on Tuesday, from seven. The kettle will be on. Our [hours and how to find us](/visit/) are on the visit page.
