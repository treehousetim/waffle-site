Every portrait sitting starts with the same email from someone in the family: "What should we wear?" It is a good question, because clothes really do change how a portrait looks, and a few simple choices make the difference between a photograph you frame and one you file. Here is what we tell everyone.

## Wear what you feel like yourself in

Before any rules: if you would never wear it on an ordinary good day, do not wear it for your portraits. You will look slightly uncomfortable in every frame, and the photographs will not look like you. Your favourite jumper is better than a new outfit you are unsure of.

## Colours

Soft, natural colours photograph beautifully outdoors: cream, oatmeal, sage, rust, dusty blue, deep green, warm grey. They sit well with grass, autumn leaves and sea. A few things to avoid:

- **Very bright colours**, especially neon, reflect onto faces and pull the eye away from them.
- **Pure white** can lose its detail in sunlight. Ivory or cream is kinder.
- **All black**, for a family, can turn into one dark shape. One person in black is fine.

## Patterns and logos

Small, busy patterns (tight checks, thin stripes) can shimmer strangely in photographs. Larger, softer patterns, like a floral dress, are lovely. Logos and slogans date a photograph faster than anything and draw the eye straight to them, so leave them at home.

## Texture

Texture is the secret. Knitwear, linen, corduroy, a wool coat, a flowing skirt that moves when you walk: all of them make a photograph feel tactile and warm. Movement especially: a dress that catches the wind is worth a dozen poses.

## Dressing a family, without matching

Matching outfits look like a uniform. Instead, choose a small palette of three or four colours that sit well together and let everyone wear something from it. The Harlows, all eleven of them, chose greens, creams and denim, and each person wore something different. In the photographs they look like a family, not a team.

A useful test: lay everyone's outfits out on the bed together and take a photo on your phone. If one item jumps out, swap it.

## Shoes

Our sittings are outdoors: an orchard, a beach, a lane. Wear shoes you can walk on grass and sand in, and bring the smart ones in a bag if you would like them for a few frames. Small children are happiest barefoot, where it is safe.

## For couples

Think of yourselves as a pair rather than as matching. If one of you is in a pattern, the other might be in a plain colour from it. Rosa and Ben came to their anniversary sitting in a floral dress and a soft pink shirt, and it worked because neither was trying too hard.

## Bring a spare

A second outfit is welcome on a [sitting](/portfolio/portraits/), if you would like variety; there is usually time for one change. Bring a hairbrush, lip balm, tissues and a snack for anyone under ten. We will bring the blanket.
