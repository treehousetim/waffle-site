If you have looked at wedding photographs for more than an evening, you have seen golden hour, even if you did not know its name. The long shadows, the warm skin, the hair lit from behind like a halo. It is not a filter. It is the sun, low in the sky, and it happens every clear evening whether anyone is getting married or not.

## What it actually is

When the sun is high, its light comes straight down. It is hard and white, and it puts dark shadows under eyebrows and noses. As the sun drops towards the horizon its light travels through much more air before it reaches you. The air scatters the blue away and leaves the warm end of the light: amber, honey, rose.

Low light also comes from the side, so it models faces gently instead of flattening them, and it lets us put the sun behind you, so that it glows through your hair and veil while your faces stay soft and even.

## How long you get

"Golden hour" is generous. On the Saltgrass Coast the good light lasts roughly:

- **Midsummer:** about an hour, from 7:30 to 8:30 p.m.
- **Spring and early autumn:** forty-five minutes or so.
- **Late autumn and winter:** half an hour at most, and it comes early, around four.

The last ten minutes before the sun touches the horizon are often the best of all. After it sets, there is a blue, quiet quarter of an hour that is lovely for a different reason: everything goes soft and still.

## The fog question

This coast has its own weather. In summer a bank of sea fog sometimes sits offshore all day and rolls in at about six, swallowing the sunset. It is not a disaster. Fog is the biggest softbox in the world, and some of our favourite photographs have been made in it. But if a clear sunset matters to you, a venue a few miles inland, in the hills, usually sits above the fog.

## Twenty minutes is enough

You do not need to vanish from your wedding for an hour. We ask for twenty minutes, and we find them in the lull between courses at supper. Your guests are eating and talking; you were going to be sitting still anyway.

We walk you somewhere close by that we have already scouted. We give you something to do rather than poses to hold: walk to that tree and back, tell each other what you remember about this morning. Then we bring you back before the pudding.

## When the weather does not play along

Sometimes the sky is grey from dawn to dusk. Then the light is even and soft all day, and there is no golden hour to chase, so we are free to take you out whenever suits the day. If it rains, [we have a plan for that too](/journal/rain-on-your-wedding-day/).

The one thing we would ask, whatever the forecast: do not schedule the speeches or the first dance at the exact moment of sunset. Give it to yourselves instead.
