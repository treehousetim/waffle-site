A "first look" is when the two of you see each other, privately, before the ceremony, rather than for the first time as one of you walks down the aisle. It has become popular, and couples often ask us whether they should. We have photographed hundreds of both, and our honest answer is that either is wonderful. Here is how to choose.

## The case for a first look

The main reason is time. If you see each other an hour or two before the ceremony, we can take most of your portraits together, and some of the wedding party photographs, before any guests arrive. After the ceremony you go straight to your guests and the drinks, instead of disappearing for half an hour.

It matters most in winter, when the light goes early. A December wedding with a three o'clock ceremony may have almost no daylight left by the time the confetti is over. A first look at one o'clock gives you portraits in good light.

The second reason is nerves. Plenty of couples find that seeing each other early settles them. You get to talk, hold hands, laugh at how nervous you were, and then walk into the ceremony together in spirit, if not in fact.

## The case for the aisle

There is nothing quite like the moment at the top of the aisle. The doors open, the music starts, and one of you sees the other for the first time, in front of everyone who loves you. We will be there for it, one of us at the front for the face waiting, one at the back for the walk.

If that moment matters to you, keep it. A first look does take a little of its surprise. Some couples feel it is worth it; some feel strongly that it is not.

## What each does to the day

With a first look, the timeline looks something like: getting ready, the first look, portraits and wedding party, a rest, the ceremony, family photographs, drinks with your guests, and a short golden hour session at supper.

Without one: getting ready, the ceremony, family photographs, then the two of you for twenty minutes while guests have drinks, and golden hour later. Both work. The second asks a little more of your guests' patience.

## A third option

More couples now choose a "first touch" or a "first read": you stand on either side of a door or a tree, holding hands without seeing each other, and read each other a letter. Nobody sees anybody; there are a lot of tears. Then you see each other, as usual, at the aisle. It gives you the calm of a first look and keeps the surprise.

## How to decide

Ask yourselves two questions. First, how much daylight will you have after the ceremony? If the answer is "not much", a first look is worth serious thought. Second, which moment would you be sadder to lose: the quiet one alone, or the public one at the aisle?
