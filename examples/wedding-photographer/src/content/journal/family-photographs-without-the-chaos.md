The family photographs are the part of a wedding nobody looks forward to, and the part everyone is glad of twenty years later. They are also the part most likely to make a day run late. With a little planning they take twenty minutes and feel almost fun. Here is how we do them.

## Write a list, and keep it short

Send us a list a fortnight before the day: each group on its own line, with names. "Bride with grandparents (Rose and Albert)" is much more use to us on the day than "grandparents".

As a guide, eight to twelve groups is plenty for most weddings. Each one takes about two minutes, which sounds quick until you have twenty-five of them and it is suddenly an hour. If a group is really for one person's sake ("Dad with his brothers"), it can often happen later in the evening, informally, and look better for it.

## Order it so people can leave

We start with the biggest groups and work down, and we arrange it so that people can drift back to the drinks as soon as they are done:

1. Everyone, both families together.
2. One side's extended family, then the other's.
3. Immediate families.
4. Parents.
5. Grandparents.
6. The wedding party.

Grandparents and anyone who finds standing difficult come first, or get a chair and a glass of something while they wait.

## Choose a wrangler

Ask one person from each side who knows everyone, has a loud voice and is not shy, to round people up. They have the list; they fetch the next group while we photograph the current one. This single job saves more time than anything else on this page.

## Pick the place in advance

We will look at your venue and choose somewhere in open shade, out of squinting sun, with a plain background and enough room for the biggest group. Tell your guests where it is in the order of service or have someone announce it after the ceremony: "family photographs on the west lawn, straight after the confetti."

## The people who are missing

If someone important will not be there, or is no longer with us, tell us. We can leave a little space, or include a photograph or a keepsake, or simply be gentle with that group. You do not need to explain anything on the day; just put a note on the list.

## Children and dogs

Bring them in early, photograph them quickly and let them go. Children last about three groups before they are done, and dogs about one. A pocket of snacks helps with both.

## And then, the relief

When the last group is done we take the two of you away for ten minutes to breathe. It is often the moment the day sinks in. Then you go back to your guests, and nobody has to stand in a line for the rest of the day.

If you would like help with your list, send it over once you have booked. We have seen a few hundred, and we are happy to say which groups you can cut.
