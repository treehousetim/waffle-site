It rains on the Saltgrass Coast. Not every day, and less in summer than you might think, but often enough that every couple asks us about it, usually more than once. Here is what actually happens if it rains on your day, and how we plan for it so that you do not have to worry.

## We plan for rain at the start, not on the morning

When we write your timeline, we write a wet version of it too. For each part of the day we decide where it would happen indoors or under cover: the ceremony, the family photographs, the twenty minutes with the two of you. We walk your venue with that in mind, looking for a porch, a barn door, a big window, a covered terrace.

On the morning we check the radar together and choose. Often we use both plans: the ceremony inside, then out for portraits in a dry half-hour that arrives, as it so often does on this coast, just before supper.

## Rain is better light than you think

Overcast skies are a giant, even softbox. Skin looks smooth, colours are rich, and nobody squints. The world after rain is shiny and dark and saturated. Some of the photographs couples love most from their whole gallery were taken under an umbrella, laughing, in a downpour.

What rain is bad for is time. Everything takes longer when people are dodging puddles, so we build in slack.

## What we bring

- Two large clear umbrellas, which let light through to your faces. (Black or patterned ones throw a heavy shadow. If you are buying umbrellas for guests, buy clear ones.)
- Towels for the car, and a few for shoes.
- Lights, so that a dark barn or an evening dance floor still looks warm.

## What you might bring

- Shoes you can walk on wet grass in, for the walk to the portraits. Wellingtons under a wedding dress are a coastal tradition at this point.
- A warm layer: a wool shawl, a jacket, a blanket for the car.
- Patience with the timeline. A rainy day can still have its golden hour, if the cloud breaks at the right moment. We will be watching the sky.

## Elopements in the rain

Elopements are even easier, because there are no guests to move. We always plan a backup spot, nearby and sheltered, and often it is a forest. The redwoods in rain are extraordinary: the bark goes dark red, the ferns glow, and the whole grove smells of wet earth. Ines and Callum's morning in the Cathedral Grove was in a light drizzle, and you would never know.

## When it is really wild

Very occasionally the weather is genuinely dangerous: high wind on a cliff, a storm surge on a beach. Then we move, without discussion, somewhere safe, and we have done that a handful of times in twelve years. Nobody has ever regretted it.

So, if it rains: we will have a plan, and an umbrella, and probably some of your best photographs.
