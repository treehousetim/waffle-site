When couples visit a venue, they look at the view, the room and the menu. When we visit one, we look at the windows. It is not that the view does not matter. It is that a few dull, practical things decide how your photographs will look far more than the view does, and they are easy to check on a visit if you know what to look for.

## Which way does it face?

Ask where the sun sets, or stand outside and look west. A terrace that faces the sunset gives you golden hour on your doorstep. One that faces east is lovely for a morning ceremony and in shadow all evening. Neither is wrong; it changes when things should happen.

For an outdoor ceremony, it matters which way you will be standing. We would rather the sun were behind your guests or to one side than in your eyes, or behind you so that your faces are in deep shadow against a bright sky.

## Windows, ceilings and walls

Indoors, light comes from windows, so count them. Big windows along one side of a room give soft, beautiful light for getting ready and for speeches. A room with small windows or none means we will be adding light, which we can do well, but it is never quite the same.

Look up, too. Low, dark ceilings swallow light. High, pale ones bounce it around. And notice the walls: a strongly coloured wall will cast its colour onto everyone near it. A green marquee lining makes everyone look a little seasick. Ivory, wood and stone are kind.

## The getting-ready room

This one is overlooked constantly. You will spend two or three hours in it, and we will photograph a lot of that time. Ask to see it. Is it big enough for everyone? Is there a window? Is there somewhere tidy to hang a dress? A cramped, dark room with suitcases everywhere is hard to make beautiful. A bright corner and a cleared bed make all the difference.

## Somewhere for the two of you

Is there somewhere five minutes' walk away, out of sight of the guests, where we can take you for golden hour? A lawn edge, an orchard, a lane, a field gate. At Ashcombe House it is the east lawn; at the Old Net Loft it is the lane behind the harbour. Most venues have somewhere, but it is good to know before you book.

## A plan for rain

Ask what happens if it rains. Is there a covered terrace, a porch, a barn? Can the ceremony move inside, and where? A venue that has a good answer has been asked before, which is a good sign. [Our own rain plan is here](/journal/rain-on-your-wedding-day/).

## Questions worth asking before you sign

1. What time must music stop, and the last guests leave?
2. Are there any rules about photography: places we cannot go, flash in the ceremony?
3. Is the venue used by anyone else on the day?
4. Where can the photographers eat, and when?
5. Can we visit at the time of day the wedding will be?

That last one is the best of all. A venue at eleven in the morning and at six in the evening are two different places. If you can, go back at the hour your ceremony will be and simply stand there for a while. You will know.
