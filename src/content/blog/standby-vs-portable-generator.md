Both keep the lights on. They differ in how much they power, who has to be home, and how much they cost.

## Portable generators

- Lower cost, and useful away from the house.
- Someone has to wheel it out, fuel it and start it, in the storm.
- Must run outdoors, far from windows: carbon monoxide kills people every winter.
- Needs a **transfer switch or interlock** with an inlet box. Back-feeding through an outlet with a double-male cord can electrocute a utility lineworker.

## Standby generators

- Permanently installed, running on natural gas or propane.
- Starts by itself within seconds of an outage, whether you are home or not.
- Can run the whole house, central air included, with load management.
- Tests itself weekly and needs a yearly service.

## Which to choose

If outages are rare and short, a portable unit with a properly installed inlet is good value. If you work from home, rely on a sump pump, or have someone on medical equipment, a [standby generator](/services/generators/) is worth the cost.
