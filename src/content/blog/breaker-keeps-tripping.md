A breaker trips for one of three reasons, and each one looks a little different.

## An overloaded circuit

The breaker trips after things have been running for a while, usually when you add one more load: the space heater, the hair dryer, the microwave. Nothing smells or sparks. Move something to another circuit and the problem goes away.

That is the breaker doing its job. If it happens every day, the room needs another circuit.

## A short circuit

The breaker trips **instantly**, often with a pop, the moment you switch something on or plug something in. Unplug that device and reset: if it holds, the fault is in the device. If it trips again with everything unplugged, the fault is in the wiring.

## A ground fault

GFCI breakers and outlets trip when current leaks to ground, often through water. Kitchens, bathrooms, garages and outdoor outlets are where you will see it. A GFCI that trips after rain usually means water is getting into an outdoor box.

## When to stop resetting

- The breaker trips again straight away with nothing plugged in.
- You smell burning, or the panel or an outlet is warm.
- The breaker will not reset at all.

Switch that circuit off and [book a repair](/services/repairs/). A breaker that is reset over and over is a breaker being asked to fail.
