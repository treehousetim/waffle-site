An arc fault is electricity jumping a gap: a nail through a cable, a loose connection in a backstabbed outlet, a cord pinched under furniture. Arcs are hot enough to ignite wood and insulation, but they often draw too little current to trip an ordinary breaker.

## How an AFCI helps

An arc-fault circuit interrupter watches the shape of the current, not just its size. The ragged signature of an arc trips it.

## Where the code requires them

Most new or extended circuits feeding living spaces, including bedrooms, living rooms, kitchens and laundry rooms. When we add or extend a circuit in an older home, AFCI protection usually comes with it.

## Nuisance trips

Some older motors, treadmills and vacuum cleaners can confuse an AFCI. More often, a "nuisance" trip is a real loose connection somewhere on the circuit. We test before blaming the breaker.
